Both projects end in a live defence. This is not a demo — the demo is assumed to work. The
defence is about whether you understand what you built.

## Format

| | Midterm | Capstone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walkthrough | 7 min | 12 min |
| Hostile Q&A | 8 min | 13 min |
| Panel | 2 reviewers | 3 reviewers |

## What "hostile" means

Not rude — **unsatisfied**. Every answer gets a follow-up until you either reach the real
reason or say "I don't know". Saying "I don't know, and here's how I'd find out" scores well.
Bluffing scores zero, every time, and is obvious within one follow-up.

## The five questions behind every other question

<Steps>
  <Step title="Why this, and not the obvious alternative?">You must be able to name the alternative and why you rejected it.</Step>
  <Step title="What breaks first?">Every system has a first failure. If you don't know yours, you haven't load-tested it.</Step>
  <Step title="What does it cost?">Per request, per user, per month. With the number that dominates identified.</Step>
  <Step title="How do you know it works?">Point at the measurement, not at the demo.</Step>
  <Step title="What would you do with three more months?">Tests your judgement about what actually matters next.</Step>
</Steps>

## How to prepare

Record yourself answering all five, in five minutes, without notes. Watch it back. The place
you hesitate is the place the panel will push, without exception.
