## Deliverables

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1 · Design document" icon="file-text">
    Problem, users, the decisions the system makes autonomously, the autonomy boundary, and a
    blast-radius analysis: what is the worst thing this can do, and what stops it?
    Include the hand-drawn architecture sketch **and** the final version — both, so the thinking shows.

    **Accepted when:** every node carries an autonomy level (suggest / approve / auto) with a written
    justification; the blast-radius table has six columns (worst outcome, what stops it, what detects
    it, how it is undone, undo time, likelihood) and **"what stops it" names code, not a prompt
    instruction**; there is an explicit never-automate list with reasons; and the rollout plan states
    a numeric gate for each autonomy transition *before* launch. See Week 32 M5.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="2 · LangGraph multi-agent implementation" icon="network">
    Typed state, durable Postgres checkpointing, human-approval interrupts before destructive
    actions. Crash-resume proven by a test that kills the process at random points.

    **Accepted when:** `make chaos N=20` reports `duplicate_effects: 0` and `lost_runs: 0`; every
    idempotency key is **derived** from `(thread_id, intent)` rather than random; every accumulating
    state field has an explicit reducer, and any field a retried node can write de-duplicates on a
    natural key; and the replay cost overhead is stated as a number. See Week 32 M3.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="3 · Hybrid RAG on pgvector" icon="search">
    BM25 + dense + cross-encoder re-ranking, with a published ablation table on your own eval set.

    **Accepted when:** the ablation has at least four rows (BM25 alone, dense alone, hybrid, hybrid +
    re-rank) with recall@k on a labelled set of at least 60 query-document pairs, and the chosen `k`
    is justified by a measurement rather than a default. Reference points from Week 32 M5: 0.71 /
    0.68 / 0.84 / 0.91.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="4 · A locally-served fine-tuned SLM" icon="cpu">
    QLoRA-tuned, served through vLLM for at least one specialized step, with the cost/quality
    justification versus a hosted model.

    **Accepted when:** the crossover arithmetic is shown — fixed GPU cost, throughput, marginal cost
    per run, break-even volume — **including redundancy, the amortized fine-tune, and ongoing
    ownership**, which together moved our own crossover by 2.3×. A correct conclusion of "we are
    below the crossover, so we use the hosted model for now, and here is the date we revisit" scores
    full marks. See Week 34 M3.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="5 · Custom MCP server" icon="plug">
    Real capabilities, a documented and tested trust boundary, and an audit log.

    **Accepted when:** read and write capabilities are in **separate servers with separate
    credentials**, and the write server is reachable only from the node behind the approval gate; any
    shell access is allow-listed at both program and flag level, sandboxed with no network and a
    scrubbed environment, output-capped and timed out; path containment resolves *before* it checks;
    and the append-only audit log answers "what did this server do for this user in this window" as
    a single query. See Week 30 M4.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="6 · Eval suite in CI" icon="circle-check">
    Assertions + a judge validated against human labels + RAG metrics, gating every merge.

    **Accepted when:** at least 100 cases derived from a documented error taxonomy built by reading
    real outputs; the judge's Cohen's kappa against **50 human labels** is reported alongside the
    human–human ceiling, with a shipping threshold stated *before* measurement (a judge below its
    threshold must not gate); per-component RAG metrics are present; and `EVALS.md` states the
    smallest change the suite can detect and what it cannot see. See Week 33 M1–M3.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="7 · Guardrails and a red-team report" icon="shield">
    Input moderation, output checks, and at least 15 documented attack attempts with mitigations.

    **Accepted when:** the red-team report has **two separate columns — compliance and catastrophic
    impact** — because the target is not an unfoolable model; every mitigation is labelled as
    reducing probability or reducing consequence, and at least one reduces consequence; the guardrail
    layer is measured against three sets (100 attacks, 100 benign, **100 benign lookalikes**) with a
    stated false-positive budget; and at least one attack is documented as still working, with an
    accepted-risk justification. See Week 33 M4–M5.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="8 · Full observability" icon="activity">
    OpenTelemetry → Langfuse, per-step cost attribution, dashboards, alerts on quality not just errors.

    **Accepted when:** a test proves the traces are trees (one trace id, one root, and correct
    nesting across any thread or queue boundary); costs include cache-creation and cache-read tokens
    at 1.25× and 0.1×; PII is redacted in a span processor rather than at call sites; and at least
    one alert fires on a **quality** signal rather than an error signal. See Week 34 M1–M2.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="9 · Kubernetes deployment" icon="ship">
    Staged rollout with automated eval gates and scheduled nightly evals.

    **Accepted when:** the deployable unit versions code, prompts, **a pinned model version** (not a
    moving alias), retrieval config and eval suite together, and all of them appear in every API
    response; the canary has three gates (offline suite, an online quality metric, cost per run); the
    termination grace period exceeds your longest run; and scheduled evals run against **production
    configuration**, or they cannot detect the thing they exist to detect. See Week 34 M4–M5.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="10 · Operations artifacts" icon="book">
    Runbook, total cost of ownership analysis, and a post-mortem from a game day you ran yourself.

    **Accepted when:** the TCO includes **engineer-days**, not only API spend (ours was 71% API and
    21% people, and the people line is the one that decides whether a system survives); the game day
    covers four conditions — provider outage, rate limiting, a silently degraded model, a
    prompt-injection attempt — diagnosed from telemetry alone with detection times published; and the
    post-mortem contains a "why detection failed" section, which is where the learning is. See
    Week 34 M4–M5.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

</Warning>

## The three-week shape

The capstone runs alongside Weeks 30–32. It is not a fourth thing to do in week 33.

| Week | Alongside | What you produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **30** | Agents II | The design document and blast-radius analysis — **before any capstone code**. The graph, the state schema, the autonomy table. Get the design reviewed by someone who would have to own the systems it touches; they will find a row in your blast-radius table that is wrong. |
| **31** | Evals & defensive AI | The eval suite from your own system's failures, the validated judge, and the red-team report. This is the week that decides your grade, because four of the ten deliverables and 30 of the 100 points depend on measurement. |
| **32** | LLMOps | Instrumentation, the cost-reduction experiment, the game day, the TCO, and the poster. |
| **Defence** | after Week 34 | 25 minutes plus hostile questions. |

**The two most common failure modes**, both of which we would rather you avoided than learned from:
building for two weeks before writing the design document, so the blast-radius analysis becomes a
description of what you happened to build; and leaving the eval suite until the end, which caps you
at 60 and — worse — means every number in your report is unverified.

| | Estimate |
| --- | --- |

| Local GPU (Llama Guard, the fine-tuned SLM) | Free on a laptop at 4-bit; roughly $12 of cloud GPU if you rent one for the fine-tune |
| Time | 60–90 hours over three weeks, on top of the weekly modules |

If you are heading past $60 of API spend, stop and read Week 34 M3. Something is running the live
model where a mock or a cheap model would do, and finding it is itself part of the assignment.

## The 25-minute defence

Five questions decide the grade. Here is what a strong answer contains — none of these can be
answered by describing your architecture.

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Why this architecture?" icon="drafting-compass">
Not what you built — **what you rejected and why.** The strongest answers name a simpler design that
would have worked, and the specific property that ruled it out. If everything in your system is
there because it was on a list, say so; a defended "we used a graph because we needed checkpointed
approval gates, and a chain would have done for two of the five paths" beats a tour of components.
Bonus: name a place where you deliberately used *less* agency than you could have.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What breaks first?" icon="triangle-alert">
A specific component, a specific load or input, and how you know. "Retrieval, at about 40,000
documents, because our HNSW `ef_search` is tuned for the current corpus and recall@5 falls from 0.91
to 0.78 in a synthetic 4× test" is an answer. "Probably the model" is not. Follow it with the
detector: what fires, and how long before someone notices — a number, from your game day.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What does it cost?" icon="banknote">
**Cost per successful task**, not per call, with the waterfall showing which levers bought what.
Then total cost of ownership including engineer-days. Expect to be asked what a *failure* costs in
human time, and whether that changes which configuration is optimal — it usually does, and knowing
it is the difference between optimizing model spend and optimizing the business.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="How do you know it works?" icon="clipboard-check">
The eval suite, its size, where the cases came from, the judge's measured agreement with human
labels and the human–human ceiling, **and the smallest change the suite can detect**. Then the
honest half: the offline/online gap, and what the suite cannot see. A candidate who volunteers the
limitations of their own instrument is trusted on everything else they say.
</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What would you do with three more months?" icon="calendar">
A ranked list with reasons, not a wish list. The best answers are mostly *removal* and
*measurement* — retiring a component that did not earn its cost, closing the offline/online gap,
getting a detector for the failure mode you currently cannot see. Naming the thing you would delete
is the single most senior-sounding answer available here.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
**Hostile Q&A is part of the format and it is not personal.** The questions that come up most:
"what stops a customer's ticket from causing that tool to run?", "your judge said quality improved —
did output length change?", "you say no quality regression; what size of regression could your suite
actually detect?", and "who owns this in eight months?" All four have good answers if you did the
work, and none of them can be improvised.
</Note>
