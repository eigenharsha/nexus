## Weekly lab rubric

Scored 0–3 on four dimensions (see [verifying your work](/labs/verify)). Grade **Explanation**
hardest — it's the dimension that predicts interview performance and nearly everyone under-invests in it.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | Would pass review at a good company, unchanged |
| 2 | Correct and tested; a reviewer would ask for changes |
| 1 | Works on the happy path |
| 0 | Doesn't meet the spec |

## Draw-from-memory (weekly, graded)

| Score | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | All components, correct relationships, correct labels, and the failure point marked |
| 2 | All components and correct relationships |
| 1 | Recognizable but with a wrong relationship |
| 0 | Not attempted or unrecognizable |

A learner scoring 1 or less two weeks running is not behind on effort — they're behind on the
prerequisite. Send them back one week, not forward.

## Defence scripts

Ask the same five questions of everyone, then follow up until you reach the real reason or an
honest "I don't know".

<Steps>
  <Step title="Why this, and not the obvious alternative?">Follow up: what would change your mind?</Step>
  <Step title="What breaks first?">Follow up: at what load, exactly? Show me.</Step>
  <Step title="What does it cost?">Follow up: what dominates that number, and how would you halve it?</Step>
  <Step title="How do you know it works?">Follow up: what would a failure look like that your measurement would miss?</Step>
  <Step title="What would you do with three more months?">Follow up: why that first?</Step>
</Steps>

**Scoring the defence:** "I don't know, and here's how I'd find out" scores 2/3. Bluffing scores
0, and is obvious within one follow-up. Say so, kindly, and move on.
