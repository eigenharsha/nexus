<Note>Each week gets four sessions. The page teaches the content; live time is for drawing,
debugging and defending. Never read Layer 1 aloud.</Note>

## The template

| Session | Minutes | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kickoff | 45 | 10 framing · **25 drawing the hero diagram live, including a false start** · 10 the lab as a ticket |
| Clinic | 60 | 45 live debugging of submitted code · 15 the mistake that recurred most |
| Depth | 45 | 20 the Layer 3 numbers · 15 the paper's one figure · 10 the stretch challenge |
| Review | 30 | 10 draw-from-memory check · 20 the three hardest assessment questions |

## The weeks that need extra live time

| Week | Why | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First contact with pointers and the terminal | Two extra clinics. Expect 30% to be stuck on environment setup alone. |
| 4 | Concurrency breaks people's mental models | Draw the event loop three times, in three different ways. |
| 8 | Isolation levels are genuinely hard | Run the two-session anomaly demo live, on a projector, slowly. |
| 11 | The maths wall — the biggest drop-off point in the course | Split the room by comfort. Do not let anyone skip the gradient derivation. |
| 19 | Leakage is invisible until someone shows you | Live-plant a leak in a learner's own notebook and let them find it. |
| 21 | Backprop by hand | 90 minutes, on paper, no laptops open. |
| 25 | Attention | Draw the attention matrix by hand for a four-word sentence before showing any code. |
| 29–30 | Agent design is judgement, not syntax | Critique real designs live; the interesting failures are architectural. |
| 31 | Red-teaming | Run it as an adversarial session — learners attack each other's systems. |

## Cold-calling

Cold-call on **predictions**, never on definitions. "What do you think this prints?" is
productive for everyone in the room. "What is a hash table?" humiliates one person and teaches nobody.
