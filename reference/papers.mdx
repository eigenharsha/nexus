Nothing is cited here that the authors haven't read. Each entry says **what to take from it** —
because "read the attention paper" is useless advice and "read section 3.2 to see why the
√d_k scaling exists" is not.

<Note>Papers marked **★** are the ones worth reading end to end. The rest: read the abstract,
the one figure named, and the section named.</Note>

## How to read a paper (Week 11 onward)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Three passes, not one">Abstract + figures + conclusion. Then the method. Then the maths, with a pen.</Step>
  <Step title="Find the one figure">Nearly every paper has a single figure carrying the idea. Redraw it by hand — that's your module diagram.</Step>
  <Step title="Reproduce the smallest claim">One number, one plot. If you can't reproduce anything, you haven't read it yet.</Step>
  <Step title="Write the one-sentence takeaway">If you can't, read it again. That sentence is what belongs in your notes.</Step>
</Steps>
