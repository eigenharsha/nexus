## The story this week tells

Put four points on a page — ticks in two opposite corners, crosses in the others — and try to
separate them with a single straight line. You cannot. And every model in Week 17 was, in the
end, a straight line.

This week is the escape, and it is smaller than its reputation: stack the lines, and **bend the
result between them**. That one bend is the whole of deep learning's power. The rest is working
out who is to blame for the error, learning to read a training curve like a doctor reads a
chart, and picking up the library the field runs on.

## Why this week is the hinge of the course

Weeks 17–20 taught you models that fit in your head: a linear regression has one closed-form
solution, a decision tree is a nested `if`. From this week on, every model you touch is a
composition of differentiable functions trained by gradient descent — CNNs in Week 22,
Transformers in Week 25, LoRA fine-tunes in Week 26. All of them run the same loop:
forward pass, loss, backward pass, optimizer step.

## Where this week's numbers come from

Read [About the numbers](/start/about-the-numbers) once before you trust any figure on these pages. **All three of this week's
"reference machines" are modelled** — none of them was run. The authors had no CUDA GPU, no cloud
instance, and no PyTorch installed at all, so the training curves and timings below describe what
these machines would plausibly do rather than what they did.

What you *can* trust completely is the **derived** material: the VRAM arithmetic in Module 4, the

the hardware.

For everything else, trust the *ratio* the lesson is teaching, not the decimal. The lab hands you
the harness: your own MNIST run on your own laptop is worth more than any number on this page.
