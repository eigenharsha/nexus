## The ticket

> **NEX-2007 — The review queue is full and the losses are not going down**
> **Reporter:** Ana, Payments Risk
> **Priority:** P0
>
> We block on a rules engine. It catches about half the fraud and it sends us roughly 900
> transactions a day to review manually. A reviewer handles about 40 an hour and costs us
> **€28/hour loaded**, so a review is about **€3**. When we miss a fraud we eat the transaction
> amount — our median fraud is about €19 but the mean is about €100, and the largest last
> quarter was €1,219. That gap is the whole problem: the money is in the tail.
>
> I have budget for **one reviewer**, so about 320 reviews a day, and I need to know whether
> that is the right number.
>
> Give me: what we would catch at 320 reviews a day, what it saves in euros, and what would
> change if I hired a second reviewer. And I need to be able to hand the model to the platform
> team without them needing you every time they upgrade something.

Note the two halves. The modelling half is one afternoon. The half the ticket actually turns on — a cost matrix where `c_FN` is **the transaction amount, not a constant**, and a handover that survives an upgrade — is Modules 4 and 5.

## The data

The standard credit-card fraud dataset: 284,807 European card transactions over two days, 492 frauds.

```python
from sklearn.datasets import fetch_openml
d = fetch_openml("creditcard", version=1, as_frame=True, parser="auto")
df = d.frame
y = df.pop("Class").astype(int).to_numpy()
print(df.shape, y.sum(), f"{y.mean():.4%}")
```

```
(284807, 29) 492 0.1727%
```

Twenty-eight features `V1`–`V28` are anonymised principal components; `Amount` is the only raw feature. Fraud is **1 in 579**.

<Warning>
Two properties of this dataset shape the whole lab. **`V1`–`V28` are already PCA components**, so Module 1's encoding work and Module 2's text work do not apply — the feature engineering available to you is on `Amount` and on any temporal structure you can recover. And **the rows are time-ordered** in the original file even though the OpenML version drops the `Time` column, so if you can recover ordering you should validate forward (Week 19 Module 3). Say which you did.
</Warning>

### The progression

| Step | Model | Average precision | ROC-AUC | Brier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | majority baseline | 0.00174 | 0.50000 | 0.0017350 |
| 0b | `Amount` alone | 0.00199 | 0.46493 | — |
| 1 | logistic regression (standardised) | 0.77456 | 0.96981 | 0.0006587 |
| 2 | XGBoost, `max_depth=4`, early-stopped at 276 trees | **0.87315** | **0.97427** | **0.0003953** |

(75/12.5/12.5 stratified split at `random_state=42`: 170,884 train with 295 positives, 56,961 validation with 98, 56,962 test with 99. scikit-learn 1.9.0, XGBoost 2.1.4, fit time 3.1 s.)

**Compare the two right-hand columns.** ROC-AUC separates logistic regression from XGBoost by **0.0045** — nothing. Average precision separates them by **0.0986**, a 12.7% relative improvement. On a 0.17% positive rate, ROC-AUC is the wrong headline and Week 19 Module 1 explains why. If your report leads with AUC you have chosen the metric that hides your own result.

### The cost matrix is not constant

This is what makes the lab different from a textbook exercise. Missing a €4 fraud and missing a €1,809 fraud are not the same mistake, so the cost-optimal threshold is not a single number derived from two constants — it is per-row:

```
act on row i  when   p_i * amount_i  >  (1 - p_i) * c_FP
```

which rearranges to a **per-row threshold** `t_i = c_FP / (c_FP + amount_i)`. A €5 transaction needs `p > 0.375`; a €1,000 transaction needs `p > 0.003`.

Reference numbers on the 56,962-row test set (fraud amounts there: median €19.04, mean €99.74, max €1,218.89):

| Threshold | How chosen | Realised test cost | FP | FN |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | the library default | €2,590 | 10 | 21 |
| 0.016813 | **argmin on the test set** | **€2,467** | 94 | 10 |
| 0.222664 | argmin on **validation**, applied to test | €2,588 | 12 | 17 |
| per-row `c_FP/(c_FP + amount_i)` | closed form, no tuning | **€2,583** | 127 | 18 |
| — | no model at all | €9,874 | — | — |
| — | review every transaction | €170,589 | — | — |

Three things worth noticing before you start, and each is a marking point.

**The test-set argmin (€2,467) is not an achievable number.** Choosing the threshold on the data you then report is the selection bias from Week 19 Module 3. The honest global answer is €2,588 — the threshold chosen on validation. Report both and label them.

**The per-row threshold wins, and barely: €2,583 against €2,588, a saving of €5 (0.2%).** It is theoretically correct and practically almost irrelevant here, because the loss is dominated by a handful of large frauds that any sensible threshold catches. Measuring that and saying so is worth more than a confident claim either way. The per-row thresholds themselves span 0.000232 to 1.0 with a median of 0.1209 — a huge range that produces a tiny effect.

**At `c_FP = €10` the cost-optimal threshold is 0.615 — above 0.5.** A €10 review is expensive relative to a €19 median fraud, so the arithmetic pushes the other way. "A rare class means a low threshold" is not a rule; it is a consequence of a cost ratio, and here the ratio inverts it.

### Recall at a fixed false-positive budget

The ticket's actual question. Reference:

| FP budget (test set) | Threshold | Recall | Precision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | 0.032571 | 0.8788 | 0.6397 |
| 100 | 0.015460 | 0.8990 | 0.4709 |
| 200 | 0.008264 | 0.8990 | 0.3080 |
| 500 | 0.003389 | 0.9091 | 0.1528 |

**Recall barely moves from 50 to 500 false positives — 0.879 to 0.909 — while precision falls by a factor of four.** That is the answer to "should I hire a second reviewer", and it is no: doubling the queue buys about 2 percentage points of recall. Deliver that sentence.

  <Tab title="hard">

**A recall target at a fixed FP budget, drift monitoring, upgrade-proof serialisation, and sub-10 ms end to end.**

| # | Criterion | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | **Recall ≥ 0.90 at ≤ 100 false positives** on the held-out test set | threshold fixed on validation, not test |
| H2 | **Sub-10 ms per prediction including feature computation** | single row, `time.perf_counter`, p99 over 1,000 calls |
| H3 | Drift monitoring | PSI per feature and on the prediction distribution, with the noise floor measured on two halves of the reference |
| H4 | **Serialisation that survives a dependency upgrade** | save under one scikit-learn version, load and predict identically under one at least three minor versions older |
| H5 | Model card | complete, including the worst `Amount`-decile slice and the calibration statement |

**H1 is achievable but not by default** — the reference hits 0.8990 at exactly 100 FP, just under the bar. Closing the last point needs either a better model or a better use of `Amount` (which alone is worse than random, so it is the *interaction* between amount and the components that carries signal — a ratio or a per-decile calibration).

**H2 is the one that will surprise you.** Module 5 measured a single-row `Pipeline.predict_proba` at **5.127 ms**, of which 69% is the model call and most of the total is Python and pandas overhead — while the same model batched costs **0.0103 ms/row**. Ten milliseconds is comfortable if you keep the input as a validated numpy array; it is not if you build a DataFrame per request. Profile before optimising and report the breakdown.

**H4 is the module's demonstration turned into a requirement.** Reference: under scikit-learn 1.5.2, a pipeline saved from 1.9.0 gave `AttributeError` for one estimator, bit-identical predictions for another, and a maximum prediction difference of **0.152** for a third — which loaded, ran, and was silently wrong. Your artifact must not do that. Acceptable answers: ONNX, plain code plus a JSON parameter file, or joblib with exact pins **plus a golden-prediction test that fails when it breaks**. Detection is an acceptable answer if you argue for it.

<Warning>
Do not chase H1 by tuning on the test set. `tests/test_no_leak.py` runs your whole pipeline on shuffled labels and requires AP within `0.002` of the 0.0017 base rate, and `tests/test_threshold.py` asserts your threshold was selected on validation. Both are cheap to pass honestly and impossible to pass otherwise.
</Warning>

Time box: **10 hours.**

## Ship it

<Card title="This is the part people skip" icon="rocket" horizontal>
A repo, a model card, and a cost analysis in euros that Ana could take to her director.
</Card>

The cost analysis is the artifact. One page, and these are the constraints:

- **Euros, not metrics.** "At 320 reviews a day we expect to catch about 88% of fraud by value and prevent roughly €X a year" — no AP, no AUC, no thresholds.
- **The second-reviewer question answered explicitly.** The reference says doubling the queue buys about 2 recall points; say so, with the number.
- **One chart**: recall and precision against reviews per day, with the current rules engine marked as a point on it.
- **The assumption that would change the answer**, named and priced. Here it is the fraud amount distribution — a mean of €122 against a median of €22 means the saving is dominated by a handful of large frauds, and a quarter without one would look like a failure.

Tag `v1.0.0`, push, and link it. A repo with a model card and a euro figure in it reads very differently from one with a notebook.

## Common ways this lab goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AUC 0.97 reported as the headline | ROC-AUC is insensitive at 0.17% prevalence | average precision, with the 0.00174 base rate beside it |
| The reported cost is the test-set argmin | choosing and reporting on the same data | select the threshold on validation; report at that fixed value |
| SMOTE added because the class is rare | Module 4 measured every treatment as significantly worse | if you use it, show the paired bootstrap; expect to remove it |
| One global threshold | `c_FN` here is the transaction amount, which varies | per-row threshold `c_FP/(c_FP + amount_i)` |
| Threshold chosen on the test set | the argmin of a noisy cost curve is itself noisy | select on validation, report at that fixed threshold on test |
| `Amount` dropped as useless | alone it is worse than random (AUC 0.465) — but it is the cost weight | keep it as a feature *and* use it in the cost matrix |
| Recall target met at 500 FP | the budget is 100 | report the whole recall-versus-budget curve, then meet the stated budget |
| Latency measured on a batch | H2 is per request | single row, p99 over 1,000 calls |
| Golden test regenerated to make it pass | it is now a no-op | regenerate only in a reviewed commit that says why |
| Report contains "average precision" | Ana asked for euros | rewrite in transactions, reviews and money |
