## The story this week tells

To you, it is a photograph of a cat. To the machine it is twelve million numbers — and every
tool from Week 21 already applies, because a network eats numbers.

Two problems arrive with those numbers, though: there are far too many of them, and the network
has no idea that a cat two pixels to the left is still a cat. This week is the fix — one small
window, slid everywhere — and everything that follows from it, ending with the step most courses
skip: getting the trained model out of your notebook.

<Card title="This week — models that see" icon="sparkles">
  - **Watch a photograph become a spreadsheet** — and manipulate it by arithmetic
  - **Slide one window across an image** — the idea that makes vision work
  - **Turn 10,000 photos into far more** — augmentation, and the rule people break
  - **Beat your own model in twenty minutes** — by borrowing someone else's training
</Card>

## Why this week matters more than "learn CNNs"

Convolutional networks are not the frontier any more. This week is not really about CNNs; it is
about three things that transfer directly:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Transfer learning is LoRA (Module 4)" icon="git-branch">
    Frozen backbone + new head is structurally identical to frozen LLM weights + LoRA adapter.
    Same freeze/unfreeze schedule, same discriminative learning rates, same catastrophic-forgetting
    failure. Module 4 draws the mapping explicitly, term by term.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Embeddings + nearest neighbour is RAG (Module 5)" icon="search">
    An image similarity search over 50,000 images is the *same architecture* as text retrieval
    in Week 27 — encoder, index, ANN search, recall/latency trade-off. You build it here on data
    you can look at.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Mixed precision is quantization (Module 3)" icon="binary">
    FP16 overflow, `GradScaler`, BF16's wider exponent — the numerics you meet here are exactly
    the numerics of Week 26's 8-bit and 4-bit weights, one step less extreme.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Residual connections are why Transformers train (Module 2)" icon="link">
    ResNet's skip connection makes the block Jacobian `I + ∂F/∂x`. Every Transformer block has
    two of them, for exactly this reason.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The dataset

Every module uses the same one so the numbers compose: the **BCCD blood-cell images** —
12,500 augmented 320×240 JPEGs across four white-blood-cell classes (eosinophil, lymphocyte,
monocyte, neutrophil). It is a good teaching dataset because it is genuinely imperfect: the
classes are close to balanced but the augmentation is visible, two of the four classes are hard
to tell apart even for a human, and the JPEG quality varies.
