## The story this week tells

Week 17's models were slopes. But think how a loan officer really decides — not with a formula,
with questions. *Employed? For how long? Deposit over 20%?*

That is a flowchart, and a machine can learn which questions to ask. This week: grow one tree
and watch it memorise, fix that by asking a *crowd* of trees to vote, then build the model that
wins most real-world tabular problems by learning from its own mistakes — and finish with the
two techniques for data that has no labels at all.

## The through-line

**M1** builds one tree, computes a Gini split by hand, and finds its two structural weaknesses: greedy search, and axis-alignment — measured as a depth-3 tree going from 0.9105 to a perfect 1.0000 on the same data after a 45-degree rotation. It also measures the instability that motivates everything else: flip **one** training label in 398 and 3.78% of test predictions change.

**M2** fixes that instability by averaging. A 200-tree forest cuts the same perturbation's effect to 0.37%, a **10.2x** reduction, and the variance formula `rho*sigma^2 + (1-rho)*sigma^2/B` explains both why it works and why it stops. It also demolishes `feature_importances_`: a column of pure random noise takes **81.8%** of the impurity importance, and deleting it raises held-out accuracy by **7.9 points**.

**M3** combines trees the other way — in sequence, each fixing the last one's residual — and derives XGBoost's second-order objective properly, so `w* = -G/(H + lambda)` is something you can write from memory. A 31-configuration study then shows that `max_depth` moves test AUC **17x more** than `learning_rate` once early stopping is on.

**M4** removes the labels. K-Means is two trivial steps, and every one of its failures is predictable from one fact: it is a Gaussian mixture with the covariance frozen spherical. **M5** removes the dimensions, and shows exactly which claims a t-SNE or UMAP plot cannot support — measured, on constructed data where the truth is known by design.

Two forward links matter. Week 27's IVF vector index is M4's K-Means run on embeddings, and Week 27's retrieval-quality inspection is M5's workflow. Neither is an analogy.

On macOS, XGBoost and LightGBM need the OpenMP runtime: `brew install libomp`. Without it you get `XGBoostError: Library (libxgboost.dylib) could not be loaded` and nothing else works.

Every number in this week's pages was **measured** on an Apple M3 Pro (11 cores, 18 GB) with exactly these versions and Python 3.12.9 — the machine this course was written on. Absolute timings will differ on your machine; the ratios should not. The Gini arithmetic, the bagging variance formula and the `num_leaves` / `max_depth` relationship are **derived** and hold anywhere. See [About the numbers](/start/about-the-numbers).
