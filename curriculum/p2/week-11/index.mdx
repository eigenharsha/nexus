<Warning>
**Read this before you start.** Week 11 is the biggest drop-off point in the whole course. Not because
the maths is hard — because maths is normally taught symbols-first and meaning-later, and most people
who "are not maths people" are people who were taught in that order.

This week is written the other way round. Every idea starts as a picture you draw on paper. Every idea
is introduced **as the thing it becomes later** — the dot product is introduced as semantic search,
SVD is introduced as LoRA, the chain rule is introduced as backpropagation. Nothing here is
foundational-for-its-own-sake.

If you last did maths at school, you are the person this week was written for. Read Layer 1 slowly,
draw the pictures, and do not skip to Layer 2. Five days of this buys you the entire rest of the
course.
</Warning>

## The story this week tells

This is the week people dread, and it should not be. You need a handful of ideas, all of them
concrete, and every one is introduced here with a picture rather than a proof.

Two numbers on paper become an arrow. One operation on two arrows becomes the definition of
"similar" that every recommendation and every attention weight in this course uses. A grid of
numbers turns out to be an *action*, not a table. A derivative turns out to be the steepness of a
hill. And rolling a ball down that hill turns out to be how machines learn.

<Card title="This week — the maths, from zero, with pictures" icon="sparkles">
  - **Find out where “similar” actually comes from** — one line of arithmetic
  - **See a matrix move a whole space at once** — and read it by its columns
  - **Meet the directions a transformation cannot turn** — and use them to compress
  - **Roll a ball downhill — then break it** — with steps too big and too small
</Card>

- School algebra and Pythagoras. **No prior calculus or linear algebra is assumed** — derivatives,
  vectors and matrices are all defined from scratch in Layer 1.
- Python 3.12 or 3.13, NumPy 2.x. `scikit-learn` for the sample image in Module 3.
- Paper and a pen. This is not a joke — Modules 1, 2 and 3 are drawing exercises before they are
  coding exercises, and the people who skip the drawing are the people who find Week 21 impossible.
