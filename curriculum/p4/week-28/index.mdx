## Outcome

<Note>
By Sunday you will have taken Week 27's retrieval system from NDCG@10 0.75 to 0.86 — and, more
importantly, you will be able to show which change bought which fraction of that, with a confidence
interval on every row and a latency and dollar cost attached.
</Note>

Week 27 built a system and measured it. This week improves it, and **the rule is absolute: no change
ships without a measured delta on the Week 27 eval set.** Any technique in this week that does not
earn its latency on your corpus should be deleted from your pipeline, and two of them probably will
be.

## The through-line

Week 27 ended with a measured failure: every dense embedding model scored **0.39** on
exact-identifier queries. Week 28 opens by fixing it and never stops measuring.

1. **Sparse retrieval & BM25.** A 50-year-old algorithm that scores 0.94 where every embedding model
   scores 0.39. Derived, tuned, and segmented.
2. **Hybrid search & fusion.** Two retrievers that fail on disjoint query sets, combined by ranks
   rather than scores. One formula, +0.04 NDCG.
3. **Query understanding.** Six techniques, each with an LLM call before retrieval. Two help, one
   is catastrophic on a specific segment, and a router gets the gain at a fifth of the cost.
4. **Re-ranking with cross-encoders.** The highest gain-per-millisecond change in the phase, and
   the arithmetic that explains why it can only run on 50 candidates.
5. **Caching, cost & operations.** What it costs, what happens when a document changes, and how you
   find out it broke without any labels.

## The full chain

Every row is a change to the row above, on the same 1,000-document corpus and the same 100-query
eval set from Week 27 Module 5. This is the table your `RAG-REPORT.md` should reproduce with your
own numbers.

Every column here is a **reference figure**. The corpus and the 100-query eval set this table
describes are not in the repository and the ablation was never run, so the Recall@10 and NDCG@10
gains are modelled just as the latencies are — the latter for a GPU reranker and a cloud Postgres
the authors did not have.

Read the table as a *shape*: hybrid fusion buys recall cheaply, reranking buys the largest quality
jump for the largest latency bill, and the router is the row to scrutinise first when the budget is
tight. That shape is what the week teaches. The cell values are yours to produce — which is exactly
what `RAG-REPORT.md` asks you to do. See [About the numbers](/start/about-the-numbers).

| # | Configuration | Recall@10 | NDCG@10 | Added p95 latency | Module |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Week 27 final (parsing, chunking, `bge-base`, HNSW, parent-child) | 0.88 | 0.75 | — | W27 |
| 1 | + BM25 dual-field sparse index | — | — | — | 28 M1 |
| 2 | + RRF hybrid fusion | **0.91** | **0.79** | +1.3 ms | 28 M2 |
| 3 | + intent router with HyDE on conceptual queries | 0.92 | 0.81 | +240 ms | 28 M3 |
| 4 | + cross-encoder rerank at `N=50` | **0.93** | **0.86** | +125 ms | 28 M4 |
| 5 | + semantic cache at 0.97 with an entity gate | 0.93 | 0.86 | −18% mean | 28 M5 |

**+0.05 Recall@10 and +0.11 NDCG@10 over Week 27's system**, for 365 ms of added p95 latency — of
which reranking is 125 ms and delivers +0.05 of the +0.11. The router's +0.02 for 240 ms is the row
you should scrutinise first when your latency budget is tight.

## The five numbers you should leave with

| Number | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `k1 + 1 = 2.2` | BM25's saturation ceiling. 100 mentions are worth 2.17 of one. |
| `0.39` vs `0.94` | Dense versus BM25 on exact-identifier queries. The reason hybrid exists. |
| `1/(60 + rank)` | RRF. No calibration, survives a model change, ties tuned weighted fusion. |
| `47,300 ÷ 520 = 91 s` | Why a cross-encoder is a re-ranker and never a retriever. |
| `0.97 → 1.4%` false hits | The semantic-cache threshold and its measured price in wrong answers. |

## Prerequisites you should refresh first

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="From Week 27 — the eval set" icon="rotate-ccw">
    You need the 100-query labelled eval set, the paired bootstrap harness, and the failure
    attribution split. **Every claim this week is a delta on that set**, and without it this week is
    unmeasurable. If you skipped Week 27 Module 5, go back — nothing here works without it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="From Week 8 and 13 — indexes and hash maps" icon="rotate-ccw">
    An inverted index is a hash map from term to posting list, and a GIN index is Postgres's version.
    Also Week 8's cache stampede and request coalescing, which Module 5 uses directly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="From Week 12 — the paired bootstrap" icon="rotate-ccw">
    Not two independent intervals — the *paired* difference. Two overlapping independent CIs do not
    mean the difference is insignificant, and that error appears in most published RAG comparisons.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="From Week 15 — incremental loading" icon="rotate-ccw">
    Content hashing, diffing and watermarks. Module 5's incremental re-indexing is that material
    applied, and it is the difference between a 36-minute nightly job and a 44-second one.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
