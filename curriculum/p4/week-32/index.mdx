## The story this week tells

Thirty-one weeks of building. This week is the one where it becomes somebody's job.

A customer says it was slow yesterday and you cannot say why. Finance says the bill tripled
and "we added a feature" is not an answer. The dashboard says everything is fine while one
user in twenty waits eleven seconds. And one Tuesday an email arrives saying the model you
built on is deprecated in ninety days.

None of those are bugs. They are what happens to a working system when it meets the world —
and this week is how you see inside it, pay for it, hold it steady, and hand it to whoever
comes next.

<Card title="This week — the system meets the world" icon="sparkles">
  - **Answer “why was it slow yesterday?”** — in ten seconds, from a trace
  - **See the exact prompt behind a bad answer** — not a reconstruction
  - **Halve the bill without changing the model** — because most of it is repetition
  - **Hand it over** — versioned prompts, canaries, and a runbook someone else can follow
</Card>

  - **game day** — a failure drill run on purpose, to test the defences before reality does. [Taught in Week 24](/curriculum/p3/week-24/5-operating-an-ml-service)
  - **optimizer** — the rule (like Adam) that decides how gradient nudges are applied to parameters. [Taught in Week 21](/curriculum/p3/week-21/4-optimizers-regularization-in-practice)
  - **streaming** — processing data piece by piece as it arrives, instead of loading everything first. [Taught in Week 15](/curriculum/p2/week-15/1-data-engineering-landscape)
</Accordion>
{/* RAMP:END */}

This is the last week. Nothing in it is conceptually new if you have run services before — spans,
SLOs, error budgets, canaries. Three things are different, and they are the week:

- **Latency is perceptual.** Streaming changes time-to-first-token from 4.00 s to 0.90 s and total time not at all.
- **Failures are silent.** The system returns a confident wrong answer with a 200. Your only detector is a quality metric you built.
- **The dependency changes underneath you.** A provider's model update is a deploy you did not do. A scheduled eval is the only thing that tells you.

And one unit of measure runs through all five modules: **cost per successful task, never cost per
call.** A cheaper model that fails twice as often is more expensive.

## The scope of the numbers

Weeks 29–31 measured the incident assistant on its own — roughly 75 runs a day. Week 32 operates the
whole **platform**: the incident assistant plus the support assistant, **4,200 runs a day combined**.
Where a number in this week depends on volume — trace storage, rate limits, the self-hosting
crossover, the TCO — it is at platform scale, and it is labelled as such.
