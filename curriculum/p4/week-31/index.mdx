## The story this week tells

You have built something that works. Now answer the question that ends most AI projects: **how
do you know?**

The machine gives different answers to the same question, so you cannot test it like a
function — and "it seems better" has shipped more regressions than any bug. This week is the
evidence: an eval set that turns feelings into numbers, a judge that can grade prose (once you
have checked the judge), metrics per stage so you know *which part* failed, and then the two
adversaries — the hostile user, and your own system saying what it should never say.

<Card title="This week — evidence instead of vibes" icon="sparkles">
  - **Catch the three things a prompt change quietly broke** — before your customers do
  - **Grade prose fairly** — and learn what the judge is biased toward
  - **Find the right answer built on the wrong passage** — the failure that looks like success
  - **Survive an email that gives your agent new orders** — and know why no prompt fixes it
</Card>

Two ideas carry the whole week, and they rhyme.

**On evals:** a number without a measured instrument is not evidence. An unvalidated judge is a
random number generator with good grammar; a 100-case suite cannot see a 3-point change; a
faithfulness score with no relevancy score beside it rewards saying nothing.

**On defence:** prompt injection has no complete fix, because it is the confused-deputy problem. Every
filter reduces the *probability* of compliance. Only architecture reduces the *consequence*. Our
measurements: the full guardrail layer left 4 of 15 attacks effective; removing the capability left 0.
