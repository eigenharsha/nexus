## Outcome

<Note>
By Sunday you will be able to prove your system works — with a suite, a validated judge, and
per-component attribution — and defend it against someone trying to break it, with a red-team report
and a guardrail layer whose operating point you can state in two numbers.
</Note>

Two ideas carry the whole week, and they rhyme.

**On evals:** a number without a measured instrument is not evidence. An unvalidated judge is a
random number generator with good grammar; a 100-case suite cannot see a 3-point change; a
faithfulness score with no relevancy score beside it rewards saying nothing.

**On defence:** prompt injection has no complete fix, because it is the confused-deputy problem. Every
filter reduces the *probability* of compliance. Only architecture reduces the *consequence*. Our
measurements: the full guardrail layer left 4 of 15 attacks effective; removing the capability left 0.

## The pinned stack

Written and tested against these exact versions on 2026-08-29. Weeks 29 and 30's stack still applies.

```
ragas                  0.2.10
presidio-analyzer      2.2.355
presidio-anonymizer    2.2.355
nemoguardrails         0.11.0
vllm                   0.6.3        # serving Llama Guard 3 8B locally
transformers           4.46.2
sentence-transformers  3.2.1        # bge-m3, 1024-dim (Week 27)
pytest                 8.3.3
```

Models: `claude-opus-5` for the system under test and for holistic judging;
`claude-haiku-4-5` for mechanical criteria (groundedness, claim support, injection screening) where
we measured κ 0.69 against the strong model's 0.74 at a fifth of the price;
`meta-llama/Llama-Guard-3-8B` served locally — roughly 16 GB in bf16, 5 GB at 4-bit, so it runs on a
laptop with a CPU fallback that is slow but correct.

Total API spend for a learner completing every module and the `standard` lab: **about $9**, measured.
Most of it is the judge-validation runs, which are worth every cent.

## Also this week

- **Flashcard deck:** the eval pyramid, the power table, the kappa formula and bands, the four RAG metrics and what each isolates, confused deputy, probability versus consequence, benign lookalikes, FP budget.
- **Explain it in 5 minutes:** explain to a product manager why prompt injection cannot be fixed and why the system is safe anyway. If you cannot do it without the word "prompt", you have it.
- **Peer review:** swap systems with someone and red-team theirs for two hours. Then swap eval suites and try to find a change that improves their score without improving their system. Both exercises find things the owner cannot see.
