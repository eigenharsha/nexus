## The story this week tells

Week 25 opened the locked room. This week you stop paying rent. Your friend — the model —
moves out of someone else's building and into yours: first you **shrink** them so they fit
through your door, then you decide whether to **teach** them your ways (and learn the
sticky-note trick that makes teaching affordable), then you make them **speak machine** so
your software can actually use their answers — and on the last day, the truck arrives and
they run on a machine you own.

<Card title="This week — make the model yours" icon="sparkles">
  - **Shrink an 8-billion-number brain** until it fits your hardware — and measure the cost
  - **Teach it with sticky notes** — a real fine-tune on one rented 24 GB card
  - **Force its answers into shapes software can read** — schemas and tools
  - **Serve it yourself** — and know exactly when self-hosting beats the API
</Card>

## The through-line

1. **Precision & quantization.** The same weights in fewer bits. Smaller means faster, because
   Week 25 M5 showed decode is bandwidth-bound. What does it cost in quality, and can your eval
   even detect the difference?
2. **Fine-tuning: when and why.** The decision, before the mechanism. Full fine-tuning an 8B model
   needs 132 GB. Also: fine-tuning is the wrong tool for facts, and here is the measurement.
3. **LoRA & QLoRA.** Week 11's SVD, cashed in. 132 GB becomes 14.2 GB, and a 4096×4096 matrix gets
   a 65,536-parameter patch.
4. **Structured output.** Making the model's output parseable by construction rather than by luck —
   and understanding precisely what that does and does not fix.
5. **Local & self-hosted serving.** Week 9's virtual memory, applied to the KV cache. Then the
   arithmetic that says whether you should be doing any of this instead of calling an API.

## The five numbers you should leave with

| Number | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `16 bytes/parameter` → 132.6 GB for 8B | Full fine-tuning memory. It is why LoRA exists. |
| `4096×4096 = 16.8M` vs `2×4096×8 = 65,536` | LoRA rank-8 on one matrix: 256× fewer trainable parameters. |
| `n = 50` → 95% CI ≈ ±13 points | Why most quantization comparisons you read online prove nothing. |
| Validity 81.5% → 100%, F1 0.71 → 0.74 | Constrained decoding fixes syntax, not semantics. |
| `1.22B output tokens/month`, 37% utilisation | Self-hosting break-even against a $0.60/M hosted model on one A10G. |

## Hardware and cost

<Note>
**Where this week's numbers come from.** The memory arithmetic (16 bytes/parameter, LoRA rank
counts, QLoRA totals) is **derived** and exact. Ollama figures on Apple Silicon are **measured**.
Every A10G, A100 and vLLM throughput figure is a **reference figure** — modelled from published
benchmarks and vendor specs, because the authors had no GPU. The timings in the paths below are
planning estimates of the same kind. Trust the ratios; measure your own absolutes. See [About the numbers](/start/about-the-numbers).
</Note>

## Prerequisites you should refresh first

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="From Week 11 — SVD and low-rank approximation" icon="rotate-ccw">

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="From Week 6 — Pydantic and JSON Schema" icon="rotate-ccw">

  </Accordion>

  </Accordion>

## What you ship this week

- A quantization Pareto plot with confidence intervals and a capability-stratified breakdown.
- A written fine-tune-or-not decision for a real task, with the cost/latency/quality table.
- A rank sweep with trainable parameters, VRAM, task score and a general-benchmark column.
- A model card and adapter published to Hugging Face, with a reproducible training script.
- A served endpoint with a capacity plan derived from a stated SLO, and a self-host-versus-API
  break-even analysis.

The break-even analysis is the artifact a hiring manager will actually read. It is the one that
shows you can make an engineering decision with money attached.
