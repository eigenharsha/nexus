## Four rules that do most of the work

<Steps>
  <Step title="Predict before you run" icon="crystal-ball">
    Before executing any example, write down what you think it will print. Being wrong on paper
    is how the idea sticks. Running it first and nodding along teaches you nothing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Redraw every diagram from memory" icon="pen-line">
    Once per module. On paper. Then compare with the original. This single habit outperforms
    re-reading by a wide margin, which is why it's graded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Break it on purpose" icon="hammer">
    After every working example, break it three ways and predict each error message. Half of
    engineering is reading a stack trace and knowing where to look.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explain it out loud in five minutes" icon="mic">
    To a camera, a friend, or an empty room. The place where you stumble is the place you don't
    understand yet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When you're stuck

Twenty minutes on your own, then read the "common mistakes" table, then re-read the Layer 1
explanation of whatever concept the error touches. Only then open the [solution](/start/faq).
Reading a solution before struggling with the problem feels exactly like learning and isn't.

## Spaced repetition

Every week ships an Anki deck. Ten minutes a day. The compounding on this is not subtle:
the Week 11 linear algebra is what makes Week 25's attention mechanism readable, and you will
have forgotten it by then if you don't review.
