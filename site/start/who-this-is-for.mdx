Everything in Nexus is written for two specific people. Not personas in a marketing deck —
two readers whose questions are answered on every single page.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Aarav — third-year CS student" icon="graduation-cap">
    Can use a computer. Has maybe seen a `for` loop. No job experience, no AI background.
    Has **12–15 hours a week** and a lot of energy.

    **What he needs:** to be told the truth slowly, to build things he can show people, and to
    walk into an interview able to defend a design decision instead of reciting a definition.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Meera — engineer, five years in" icon="briefcase">
    Ships backend, data or QA work every day. Strong on delivery, thin on ML internals.
    Has **5–7 hours a week**, mostly at night, and a real backlog competing for attention.

    **What she needs:** to skip what she knows, to get the trade-offs and the numbers fast, and
    to use what she learned last night in the standup tomorrow morning.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The contract

| Every page gives Aarav | Every page gives Meera |
| --- | --- |
| Every term defined at first use | Permission to skip Layer 1 entirely |
| An analogy before the formalism | The trade-offs table, up front |
| A scaffolded lab track with answers | A spec-and-tests lab track with a constraint |
| Interview framing for the topic | "Three places this shows up in your job" |
| A portfolio artifact per week | Something applicable to a real ticket this sprint |

## Who this is *not* for

Someone looking for a certificate without the work. Every week ends in a graded artifact, and
the two projects are defended in front of hostile questioning. There is no path through this
course that doesn't involve building things and being wrong in public for a while.
