## Reference solution — `P4-W32-M4`

### Stop trying to reproduce it

The measurement that should change your triage process: same input, same prompt version, same model,
20 replays → **14 distinct outputs**, and the reported failure reproduced **3 times in 20**.

At a 15% failure rate, a single clean replay happens 85% of the time. Any process that closes a
ticket on "could not reproduce" closes most real bugs. Replace reproduction with **population
comparison**: 100 traces before, 100 after, scored with the same instrument, compared with a
confidence interval. Then look for what differs between the populations — response model, prompt
version, retrieved document ids, input length distribution — rather than trying to make one case
fail.

And log enough for a **conditioned replay**: re-run with the recorded tool results substituted, so
only the model's non-determinism varies. About 60% of "the model got worse" reports turn out to be
"the world was different".

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**Obvious:** retry on 429 until it works. It converts a brief overload into a sustained one and pays
for every failed attempt — worse on latency, success rate *and* cost in our load test. Admission
control sized on the provider's limit, then queue or shed with `Retry-After`.

**Obvious:** alert on error rate. The failure mode that hurt most had a 200 status and no error
signal at all. A quality SLI on 100% of traffic is what finds it, and `gen_ai.response.model` is the
two-line change that names the cause.

**Obvious:** optimize the model call to fix latency. 52% of our p50 TTFT is the segment we do not
control, and the p95 tail was retrieval and the guardrail. Stream instead: it does not make anything
faster and it makes the wait 4.4× shorter.

## The two wrong turns worth knowing about

1. **An eval suite that scored the text and a bug that was in the parsed object.** The suite passed eleven times during a four-day degradation. Assert on every consumer of the output, not the one you find interesting.
2. **Recording `gen_ai.response.model` and not alerting on it.** The evidence was in the traces for three days while the team examined their own code. A new value appearing should be a notification.

## Grading notes

**3/3 on Operations:** SLOs with an error budget and a written policy for exhaustion; the game day
with detection times per condition; and the blind run, where ours went from 19 minutes to three hours
the first time because nobody was watching for a condition with no error signal.

**Common losses:** total latency measured and TTFT not; blind 429 retries left in place; no scheduled
eval, or one that runs against a pinned model and therefore cannot detect the thing it exists to
detect; and a post-mortem with a root cause and no "why detection failed" section, which is where all
the learning is.
