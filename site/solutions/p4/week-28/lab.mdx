## Reference solution — `LAB-P4-W28`

### What a finished `standard` submission looks like

```
repo/
├── README.md
├── RAG-REPORT.md          # ablation + cost waterfall + "what we removed and why"
├── sparse.py fusion.py transforms.py rerank.py cache.py ablate.py
├── tests/                 # 16 tests, all green
└── figures/               # segment table, gain-vs-latency scatter, depth curve,
                           # threshold sweep, cost waterfall
```

### The decisions that make it a 3/3

**Everything is a delta on the Week 27 eval set, with a paired CI.** Not an absolute number, not an
independent interval.

**At least one row of the ablation is a removal.** On this corpus, query rewriting measured +0.00 and
step-back +0.005; a pipeline that keeps both has not been evaluated, only assembled.

**Every LLM call has a deadline and a fallback to the original query**, and every prompt preserves
codes verbatim.

**The false-hit rate is hand-labelled**, stratified toward the threshold, with a risk statement.

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious submission adds every technique and reports the final number.** The ticket asked for
improvements *with what they cost*, and explicitly said four defensible changes beat eight
undefendable ones. A report where every row has a CI and two rows are removals answers the question
that was asked.

**On the `hard` track's budget:** the failure is the interesting part. The `standard` pipeline with
everything on misses 400 ms p95 because the router alone is 240 ms. Show that measurement, then show
the arithmetic that says drop the router (+0.02 for 240 ms) and keep reranking (+0.06 for 125 ms).
A submission that meets the budget by dropping reranking has optimised the wrong stage and needs to
explain why.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: turning on every technique and measuring only the end state.** You then cannot
attribute the gain, cannot remove anything, and cannot answer the ticket. Measure one change at a
time against the frozen baseline, in the order the modules present them.

**Wrong turn 2 (on `hard`): TTL-based cache invalidation.** It passes a naive freshness test and
fails the two clauses that matter — answers citing the changed document must be evicted
*immediately*, and answers citing unrelated documents must **not** be evicted. If your invalidation
is a TTL or a full flush, point 3 of the `hard` track is unattempted regardless of what the freshness
test says.

### Grading notes

**A 3/3 across all four rows** is: `hard` complete with all three budgets met, incremental
re-indexing measured against full re-ingest, invalidation proven by the eviction test, and the
stampede demonstrated and fixed; single-flight with token-checked release and follower timeout;
operating points chosen against named binding constraints with the rejected alternatives priced; and
a `RAG-REPORT.md` with paired CIs throughout, a removal section, and a risk statement a product owner
could act on.

**The three most common ways submissions lose points.** First, techniques adopted on point estimates
whose CIs include zero — this week's whole discipline is refusing to do that. Second, reranking
evaluated on Recall@10 and therefore undervalued or removed. Third, a false-hit rate that was
estimated rather than labelled; it is always reported as 0% or 100%, and neither is a measurement.
