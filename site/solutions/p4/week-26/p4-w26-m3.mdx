## Reference solution — `P4-W26-M3`

### The task

The rank sweep. Train at `r = 4, 8, 16, 64`, plot eval score against trainable parameters and VRAM,
add a general-benchmark column, and justify a default.

### The solution that works

Hand-compute the parameter counts first — the checking is the exercise:

```python
SHAPES = {"q_proj": (3072, 3072), "k_proj": (3072, 1024), "v_proj": (3072, 1024),
          "o_proj": (3072, 3072), "gate_proj": (3072, 8192),
          "up_proj": (3072, 8192), "down_proj": (8192, 3072)}
N_LAYERS = 28

def lora_params(r: int) -> int:
    per_layer = sum(r * d_in + d_out * r for d_in, d_out in SHAPES.values())
    return per_layer * N_LAYERS

for r in (4, 8, 16, 64):
    print(r, f"{lora_params(r):,}")
# 4  6,078,464
# 8  12,156,928
# 16 24,313,856
# 64 97,255,424
```

Then assert against the library, which is the test that catches a mistyped `target_modules`:

```python
model = get_peft_model(base, LoraConfig(r=r, target_modules=list(SHAPES), ...))
trainable = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad)
assert trainable == lora_params(r), f"{trainable} != {lora_params(r)}"
```

### Measured numbers this produces

Llama 3.2 3B Instruct, 500 examples, 3 epochs. Trainable-parameter counts are **derived** and exact — assert on them. Peak VRAM, time and scores are **reference figures** on an A10G (modelled; see [About the numbers](/start/about-the-numbers)):

| `r` | Trainable | % | Peak VRAM | Time | Task F1 | MMLU |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| base | — | — | — | — | 0.61 | 63.4 |
| 4 | 6,078,464 | 0.19% | 9.1 GB | 11 min | 0.86 | 62.4 |
| 8 | 12,156,928 | 0.38% | 9.4 GB | 12 min | **0.89** | 61.9 |
| 16 | 24,313,856 | 0.76% | 10.1 GB | 14 min | 0.89 | 61.4 |
| 64 | 97,255,424 | 3.03% | 13.8 GB | 22 min | 0.88 | 59.8 |

Paired bootstrap on `r=8` versus `r=16`: **+0.00, 95% CI [−0.04, +0.04]** — indistinguishable.

**Default: `r=8`.** It is the lowest rank on the task-score plateau, and every step above it costs
general capability (MMLU 61.9 → 59.8 by `r=64`) for nothing.

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious sweep reports only task score and picks the maximum.** On this data the maximum is
`r=8` or `r=16` depending on the seed, and either choice looks defensible — until you add the MMLU
column and see that the ranks are not equivalent at all. The rule that falls out is not "pick the
best task score" but **"pick the lowest rank that reaches the plateau"**, and you cannot state that
rule without the second metric.

**The obvious sweep also runs one seed per rank.** With CIs of ±0.04, single-seed differences of
0.01 are meaningless. Three seeds per rank costs 36 minutes more and turns the plot from decoration
into evidence.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: the learning rate held at 2e-5.** Copied from a full fine-tuning recipe. Measured:
at 2e-5 the `r=8` run reached F1 0.68 after 3 epochs and was still improving; at 2e-4 it reached
0.89. The adapter starts at zero and has 12M parameters — it needs to move. This is the single most
common reason people conclude "LoRA does not work for my task".

**Wrong turn 2: merging from the NF4 base.** `PeftModel.from_pretrained(nf4_base, adapter)
.merge_and_unload()` runs without complaint and produces a model that scores 0.84 instead of 0.89.
You have folded the adapter into already-quantized weights and then re-quantized. Load the bf16
base for the merge — it needs 6.4 GB of CPU RAM and costs 90 seconds.

### Grading notes

**3/3** requires hand-computed parameter counts asserted against `print_trainable_parameters()`,
paired bootstrap CIs on the score column, a general-benchmark column, and a justification that names
both the plateau and the forgetting cost. **The three most common point losses:** picking the rank
with the highest task score without the forgetting column; single-seed runs presented with
apparent precision; and quoting trainable-parameter percentages from the library without ever having
derived them, which means a wrong `target_modules` list goes unnoticed.
