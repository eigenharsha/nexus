## Reference solution — `P4-W27-M4`

### The task

On 1M vectors, produce the recall-versus-QPS curve for HNSW at five settings and IVF at five; pick
an operating point and defend it.

### The solution that works

The measurement is Module 4 Layer 2's `measure_recall.py`. The critical detail is where ground truth
comes from:

```python
# ground truth MUST be exact search over the same data, computed ONCE
cur.execute("SET LOCAL enable_indexscan = off")
cur.execute("SELECT id FROM chunks ORDER BY embedding <=> %s::vector LIMIT 10", (q,))
```

Not your labelled eval set. Those measure different things and conflating them is the most common
error in this module.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.55 | 0.3 ms | 3,400 |

**At equal recall 0.95: HNSW 980 QPS, IVFFlat 220 — 4.5×.**

Operating point: **`m=16, ef_construction=200, ef_search=100`**, recall 0.98 at p95 3.1 ms in
4.61 GB. Defence: the end-to-end p95 budget is 250 ms, of which generation takes 180 ms and
reranking 64 ms, leaving 6 ms — so **latency is not the binding constraint, memory is**, and the
right move is to spend the spare latency on recall rather than to spend 3.3 GB more RAM on `m=32`
for another 0.005.

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious defence picks the fastest configuration that clears the recall bar.** That optimises
an axis which is not binding. Identify the binding constraint first — usually memory, occasionally
build time, rarely latency once you account for generation — then push the non-binding axes to
their cheap limits. The reasoning is what is being graded, not the number.

**The obvious benchmark also runs one connection.** Report QPS at your real concurrency too;
pgvector's HNSW scans are single-threaded per query, so throughput scales with connections until
memory bandwidth saturates, and the single-connection number understates capacity by roughly the
core count.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: `maintenance_work_mem` at the default.** The 1M-vector HNSW build took **2 h 51 m**
instead of 27 minutes because it spilled to disk. There is no warning; it is simply slow. Set 8 GB
before `CREATE INDEX`.

**Wrong turn 2: benchmarking unfiltered only.** Add filtered queries and the picture changes
completely — at 3.1% selectivity with `ef_search=40`, 38% of queries returned fewer than 10 rows and
filtered recall was 0.31 against 0.95 unfiltered. A recall-versus-QPS curve with no filtered column
describes a workload you do not have.

### Grading notes

**3/3** requires both index families at five settings each, recall measured against exact search,
build time and index size, a filtered-recall column, and a defence naming the binding constraint.
**The three most common point losses:** ground truth taken from the labelled eval set instead of
exact search; no filtered queries, which is the failure the module exists to teach; and an operating
point defended by "it was fastest" rather than by identifying which resource is actually scarce.
