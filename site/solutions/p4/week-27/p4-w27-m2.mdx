## Reference solution — `P4-W27-M2`

### The task

Run the chunk-size sweep on your own corpus with paired bootstrap CIs, publish the plot, and
recommend a size.

### The solution that works

The sweep is Module 2 Layer 2's script. The piece that makes it valid is the gold-span resolver,
because chunk IDs change with every configuration:

```python
def resolve_gold(spans, chunks, min_overlap=0.5) -> list[str]:
    """spans: [(doc_id, start, end)] -> chunk ids overlapping by >= min_overlap of the span."""
    out = []
    for doc_id, s, e in spans:
        span_len = e - s
        for c in chunks_by_doc[doc_id]:
            overlap = max(0, min(e, c.char_end) - max(s, c.char_start))
            if overlap >= min_overlap * span_len:
                out.append(c.id)
    return out
```

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious sweep labels gold as chunk IDs at one configuration.** Then every other row of the
sweep scores against labels that do not exist, and the results look like noise around whichever
configuration was labelled. Character spans plus an overlap threshold is the only method that
survives re-chunking, and it is also what makes the eval set outlive the pipeline.

**The obvious recommendation picks the maximum.** With a CI of ±0.04 the maximum is not identified.
The defensible recommendation names the range the eval can resolve (256–1024), then breaks the tie
on a cost that is computable rather than estimated.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: chunking on characters instead of tokens.** `chunk_size=2000` in characters is
roughly 500 tokens in English prose, roughly 690 in Python and roughly 240 in Hindi. The same
configuration produces wildly different chunks across a mixed corpus, and the sweep becomes
uninterpretable. Chunk on `tiktoken` token counts.

**Wrong turn 2: forgetting parent deduplication.** With 256-token children inside 1,024-token
parents, the top-15 children commonly resolve to 6 distinct parents. Without dedup you paste the
same parent three times and burn 3,072 tokens for nothing — and, worse, your measured Recall@k looks
fine because the gold parent *is* in there, three times.

### Grading notes

**3/3** requires the character-span gold resolution, at least five chunk sizes, paired bootstrap CIs,
the split-rate column that explains the left half of the curve, and the generation-cost column that
breaks the tie. **The most common point loss** is a sweep with no CIs, presented as if 0.83 and 0.81
were different. Second: chunking on characters. Third: reporting the section-path prefix result as
part of the chunk-size sweep rather than as its own controlled change — it is the largest single
delta in the module and it deserves its own row.
