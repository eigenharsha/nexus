## The one rule

**Do not lecture Layer 1.** It's written down, it's better written than you will say it live,
and reading it aloud wastes the only scarce resource you have — synchronous time with learners.

Use live time for the three things the page cannot do:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Draw it live" icon="pen-line">
    Redraw the module's diagram on a board while thinking out loud, including the false start.
    Watching someone reason is the thing a static page can't give.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Debug live" icon="bug">
    Take a broken submission and fix it in front of everyone, narrating each hypothesis.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Defend live" icon="swords">
    Pick a learner's design decision and push on it until they reach the real reason.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Weekly cadence

| Session | Length | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday kickoff | 45 min | Draw the week's hero diagram live. Frame the lab as a ticket. No slides. |
| Wednesday clinic | 60 min | Live debugging of real submissions. Volunteers first, then cold-call. |
| Friday depth | 45 min | Layer 3 only. The numbers, the failure modes, the paper's one figure. |
| Sunday review | 30 min | Draw-from-memory check, then the assessment's three hardest questions. |

## Handling a mixed room

Beginners and senior engineers in the same session is a feature. Pair them on the lab: the
senior does `hard`, the beginner does `basic`, and the beginner explains their solution first.
Explaining to a senior is where beginners find out what they don't know, and seniors discover
their explanations are worse than they thought.
