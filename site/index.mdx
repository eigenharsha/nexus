## The four phases

<Steps>
  <Step title="Phase 1 · Weeks 1–8 — Foundations" icon="code">
    C and memory, the terminal, Git, object-oriented and asynchronous Python, HTTP and the
    browser, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, transactions and indexes.
    **You finish with:** a deployed, tested, concurrency-safe web service.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 2 · Weeks 9–16 — Systems & Data" icon="cpu">
    NAND gates to a working ALU, raw sockets, linear algebra and calculus, probability and
    experimentation, data structures and algorithms, ingestion pipelines and EDA.
    **You finish with:** a 16-bit ALU, a TCP server, a maths library, and an automated pipeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 3 · Weeks 17–24 — Machine Learning" icon="brain">
    Regression from scratch, ensembles, evaluation you can defend, feature engineering,
    backpropagation by hand, PyTorch and CNNs, Docker, serverless, Kubernetes.
    **You finish with:** a model serving at scale, with a midterm defence.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 4 · Weeks 25–32 — Generative AI" icon="sparkles">
    Tokenizers and Transformers from scratch, quantization and QLoRA, retrieval at 10M+
    documents, hybrid search, multi-agent graphs, MCP, evals, guardrails, observability.
    **You finish with:** a capstone you defend under hostile questioning.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The honest prerequisites

You need a computer, roughly 12–15 hours a week if you are a full-time student, or 5–7 hours a
week if you have a job, and the willingness to be bad at something for a while. You do not need
prior programming experience. You do not need a maths degree. You do not need a GPU — every
GPU lab has a documented CPU or free-Colab path, and the whole 32 weeks costs under $50 in cloud spend.
