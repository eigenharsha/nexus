## The ticket

> **QUAL-118 · Platform Engineering · P1**
>
> We shipped a prompt change on 14 August that improved the demo and regressed three real customer
> journeys. We found out from a support lead nine days later. Twice this quarter.
>
> We need to stop merging changes we cannot measure. Build an eval pipeline that runs on every
> change to a prompt, a model version, a retrieval parameter or a dependency, fails the build on a
> regression, and comments the result on the pull request.
>
> Separately: legal has asked what stops the assistant from being talked into something by text in a
> customer ticket, and what stops it emitting a customer's card number. We do not currently have an
> answer.
>
> Deliverable: the pipeline, `EVALS.md` explaining what it measures and what it cannot, and
> `RED-TEAM.md` with at least fifteen attempted attacks and what shipped for each.

**Time box, honestly:** `basic` 4 hours · `standard` 14–18 hours · `hard` a further 10–12 hours.
The `standard` track includes about three hours of *reading outputs and labelling by hand*. That is
not overhead; it is the part that makes the rest work.

<Tabs>
<Tab title="basic">

### What you build

Twenty eval cases with assertions, running locally.

**Given to you** (`labs/p4/week-31/starter/basic/`): 60 saved outputs from the Week-30 system with
their inputs and contexts, two prompt versions, and `test_basic.py`.

**You write:**

<Steps>
<Step title="Read 20 of the outputs and write what is wrong">
By hand, in your own words. Then group into at least four named categories. "Bad answer" is not a category.
</Step>
<Step title="Twenty cases from those failures">
`cases.yaml` with `input`, `must_contain`, `must_not_contain`, a taxonomy `tag`, and `origin`. At least three cases where the correct answer is a refusal.
</Step>
<Step title="Five assertions">
Valid JSON where required, required entities present, length cap, no PII, and quoted spans present in the context. All deterministic, all free.
</Step>
<Step title="The runner">
Reports per-category, returns the set of newly-failing case ids against a baseline, and exits non-zero when a previously-passing case fails.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Acceptance:** `make verify TRACK=basic` — 12 tests, no API calls, under 3 seconds. Two of them
check that an inapplicable assertion returns "not applicable" and is excluded from the denominator,
which is the subtlety of this track.

<Info>~60% of the code is written for you. TODOs are marked.</Info>

</Tab>
<Tab title="standard">

### What you build

**Part A — the suite (100+ cases)**

<Steps>
<Step title="Error taxonomy from 50 hand-read outputs">
Categories with counts, and a column saying whether each is automatable. Committed as `EVALS.md` section 1.
</Step>
<Step title="100+ cases, tiered">
Assertions on every case; reference or required-facts comparison where one exists; the judge only where nothing cheaper works. Tag every case with its taxonomy category, origin and date.
</Step>
<Step title="A validated judge">
50 human labels (yours), stratified. Report Cohen's kappa, the per-criterion confusion matrix, and the human–human kappa from at least 20 double-labelled cases. **State your shipping threshold before you measure.** Iterate the rubric and report the kappa progression.
</Step>
<Step title="RAG and trajectory metrics">
Context recall, context precision, faithfulness, answer relevancy, plus tool-call correctness and a forbidden-tools assertion that fails the build.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Part B — CI**

GitHub Actions on every change to `prompts/`, `evals/`, retrieval config, or `requirements.txt`.
Assertions on every commit; the judge on pull requests. Fails the build on any newly-failing case.
Posts a comment with the per-category table, the newly-failing ids, and the mean output length per
configuration — that last column is what catches verbosity-driven score inflation.

**Part C — guardrails**

<Steps>
<Step title="Input layer">
Llama Guard 3 8B locally, with at least two product-specific categories added. Structural checks in front of it (delimiter tags, control characters, length).
</Step>
<Step title="Output layer">
PII detection and redaction, markdown image stripping, and a groundedness check. Run them concurrently, not in series.
</Step>
<Step title="Three eval sets">
100 attacks, 100 benign, **100 benign lookalikes**. Sweep the threshold and publish the curve with a blocked-legitimate-users-per-10,000 column.
</Step>
<Step title="Fail policy and logging">
Fail-open or fail-closed, decided and written down. A blocked-request log that redacts before writing and stores text only inside the uncertainty band.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Info>Spec and tests only. `make verify TRACK=standard` runs 44 tests in under 15 seconds with no API calls.</Info>

</Tab>
<Tab title="hard">

### The constraints the standard solution fails

<Steps>
<Step title="Judge validated to a stated threshold">
κ ≥ 0.60 against your 50 human labels, with the human–human ceiling reported and position and verbosity bias measured numerically before and after mitigation. A judge below threshold must not gate the build — and saying so in `EVALS.md` is part of passing.
</Step>
<Step title="Full red-team report">
Fifteen attacks across at least eight categories (direct, authority claim, delimiter escape, encoded payload, multi-turn, via tool result, exfiltration via tool argument, exfiltration via rendered markdown, an attack on your classifier, an attack on your judge). Ten trials each. Report compliance and catastrophic impact **separately**, and label every mitigation as reducing probability or reducing consequence.
</Step>
<Step title="Runtime and cost budget">
The full suite must run in under 6 minutes and cost under $1.20 per pull request. Levers: assertions first with short-circuiting, a cheap model for mechanical criteria, exact-containment pre-filter before any judge call, and concurrency.
</Step>
<Step title="Attribution proof">
Plant five faults across the pipeline, including one invisible to every RAG metric. Report which metric moved for each and whether the suite named the right component. Publish the one it missed and the check you added.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
There is a constraint here that the `standard` solution fails: a judge built without binary criteria
lands around κ 0.44 and cannot gate; and a suite that calls the judge on every case blows both the
runtime and the cost budget. Both have to be engineered.
</Warning>

## Ship it

<Card title="This is the part people skip" icon="rocket" horizontal>
A public repository with `EVALS.md` — what the suite measures, the error taxonomy it came from, the
judge's measured agreement and its threshold, the smallest change the suite can detect, and an
honest section on what it **cannot** see — and `RED-TEAM.md` — fifteen attacks, outcomes, and each
mitigation labelled probability or consequence.
</Card>

*This is the artifact that most distinguishes a serious AI engineer from a demo builder.* The
"what it cannot see" section of `EVALS.md` is the paragraph a senior reviewer reads first.

## Verify yourself

```bash
cd labs/p4/week-31
make verify TRACK=basic       # 12 tests, no API calls, ~3 s
make verify TRACK=standard    # 44 tests, no API calls, ~15 s
make verify TRACK=hard        # 44 + judge validation + red-team suite

make evals                    # the full suite; writes evals/report.md
make judge-validate           # kappa vs your labels, per-criterion confusion, bias tests
make redteam                  # 15 attacks x 10 trials; writes RED-TEAM.md tables
make guardrail-sweep          # threshold sweep over attacks / benign / lookalikes
```

`make judge-validate` must print a kappa and your stated threshold side by side, and exit non-zero
when the judge is below threshold and still wired into the gate. That check is the point of the lab.

## Rubric

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eval design | no suite | some cases | 100+ cases from a real taxonomy, tiered, tagged | power stated, offline/online gap tracked, coverage report |
| Measurement rigour | numbers with no method | a score | judge validated with kappa | human–human ceiling, bias measured and mitigated, threshold stated in advance |
| Attribution | end-to-end only | per-component metrics | five planted faults attributed | the fault no metric can see, found and covered by a new check |
| Security | none | a moderation call | 15 attacks with outcomes | probability/consequence labelled, architectural fix shipped, egress inventory |
| Guardrail honesty | no FP measurement | FPR on benign only | lookalike set and threshold sweep | signed FP budget, per-segment FPR, latency and streaming cost measured |

Score 12+/15 before you call it done. **Measurement rigour below 2 is a fail regardless of total** —
this is the week about not fooling yourself, and an unvalidated judge gating a build is exactly the
failure it exists to prevent.
