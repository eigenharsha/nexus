## The ticket

> **NEX-4288 · Document intake · P1**
>
> Ops spends 6 hours a day re-keying supplier invoices into the ERP. We tried a hosted model with a
> prompt; it returns unparseable output on about 12% of documents and gets the line-item totals
> wrong often enough that ops now checks all of them, which was the thing we were trying to remove.
>
> Two constraints. Finance will not send supplier documents to a third-party API — this has to run
> on our own hardware. And the extraction has to be *schema-valid every time*, because the ERP
> importer is a batch job that fails the whole file on one bad record.
>
> Build it. We have one A10G. Tell us what it costs to run, and tell us honestly whether we should
> have just paid for the API.

**Time box, honestly:** `basic` 3–4 hours. `standard` 14–18 hours, of which 8 is dataset work.
`hard` a further 10–12 hours. The dataset is the long pole and it always is.

**Hardware:** `standard` needs a CUDA GPU with 16 GB for the QLoRA run — a Colab T4 free tier
completes it in 38 minutes. Everything else, including serving with Ollama and the whole of
Module 4's constrained decoding, runs on a 16 GB laptop. `hard`'s serving benchmarks need a real
24 GB card for about 90 minutes (roughly $1.50 on `g5.xlarge`).

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="basic">
    **Scaffolded. About 60% is written for you; every gap is marked `# TODO`.**

    1. **Run a provided QLoRA config and verify it loads.** `starter/train_qlora.py` ships complete
       except for the `LoraConfig`. Fill in `r`, `lora_alpha` and `target_modules`, run 200 steps on
       the 300 provided examples, and confirm `print_trainable_parameters()` reports under 1%.
       Record the number and reconcile it against your own hand arithmetic from the model's shapes.
    2. **Generate from the adapter and from the base**, on the same 20 documents, and count how many
       parse as JSON in each case. Two numbers, one paragraph.
    3. **Add constrained decoding** with the provided `Invoice` schema and re-run. Report
       schema-valid rate for all three (base, adapter, adapter + constrained) and say which of your
       remaining errors are syntactic and which are semantic.

    <Info>
    Acceptance: `make verify TRACK=basic` green. Roughly 30 lines of your own code, plus one
    paragraph of analysis that must correctly distinguish the two error classes.
    </Info>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="standard">
    **Spec and tests only. You write the implementation.**

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Build the eval set first">
        Before any training data: 100 documents with hand-checked gold extractions from
        `data/gold/`, plus your scoring script. Metrics: schema-valid rate, per-field exact-match
        F1, and **groundedness** (fraction of extracted values appearing verbatim in the source).
        Measure the base model on it. **This baseline number goes in your README** — everything
        later is a delta against it.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Build a 500+ example training set">
        From `data/raw/`. Required: a written quality rubric applied before collection, exact and
        near-duplicate removal (0.95 cosine), a 500/100/100 split with no near-duplicate crossing a
        boundary, and at least 15% of examples where at least one field is legitimately absent and
        the correct answer is `null`. Report the count at every stage of the funnel.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Design the schema">
        Pydantic, with `Optional` for everything the real world permits to be absent, a free-text
        `reasoning` field first, `Field` patterns on the formatted fields, and at least one
        cross-field validator that no JSON Schema can express. Field order must be justified in a
        comment.
      </Step>
      <Step title="QLoRA fine-tune a 3B model">
        NF4 base, double quantization, paged 8-bit optimizer, right padding, gradient checkpointing.
        Validation each epoch with `load_best_model_at_end`. Run the pre-flight check from Module 3
        before training — token-length distribution against `max_seq_length`, padding side, and
        trainable-parameter percentage.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Reach &gt;99% schema-valid on the held-out set">
        Report the path you took: fine-tune alone, fine-tune plus constrained decoding, or
        fine-tune plus repair. Show the number for each; the requirement is 99%, the deliverable is
        the comparison.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Merge, serve, integrate">
        Merge from the **bf16** base (not the NF4 one — Module 3 explains why, and the test checks
        it), serve with vLLM 0.6.2 behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and call it from the
        Week 6 FastAPI service with a `/readyz` that actually generates a token.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Info>
    Acceptance: `make verify` green, `RESULTS.md` showing baseline and final on the same 100-item
    eval with paired bootstrap CIs, and a running endpoint that `make smoke` can hit.
    </Info>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="hard">
    **A constraint the `standard` solution fails.**

    Beat the base model plus constrained decoding on **both accuracy and cost**, and prove it.

    1. **The rank sweep.** `r = 4, 8, 16, 64`. Report trainable parameters (hand-computed *and*
       printed), peak VRAM, training time, task F1 with paired bootstrap CIs, and a general
       benchmark (MMLU or IFEval) to show the forgetting. Choose a default and justify it against
       the plateau *and* the forgetting column.
    2. **The quantization Pareto plot.** Serve your merged model at 4 quantization levels; plot task
       F1 against GB with CIs as error bars and tokens/sec as point size. State which differences
       your eval size can resolve.
    3. **The serve-versus-API cost analysis.** Concurrency sweep on your own hardware, the
       concurrency that meets a p95 TTFT SLO you state up front, the `$/M tokens` curve against
       utilisation, and the break-even volume. Then answer the ticket's last question honestly.
    4. **The constraint:** your system must beat `base model + constrained decoding` on field F1 by
       a margin whose paired bootstrap CI excludes zero, **and** cost less per thousand documents at
       your projected volume. If your honest answer is that the API wins, that is an acceptable
       submission — but you must show the arithmetic, and you must show the accuracy gap that the
       cost saving is being traded against.

    <Warning>
    The `hard` track's real difficulty is that it can legitimately conclude "we should have paid for
    the API". A submission that reaches that conclusion with sound arithmetic scores higher than one
    that reaches the opposite conclusion by assuming 100% GPU utilisation.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Ship it

<Card title="This is the part people skip" icon="rocket" horizontal>
A **model card** and the **adapter** on Hugging Face, plus a reproducible training script. The model
card must state: base model and revision hash, dataset size and construction method, the rubric,
the eval set and metrics, measured lift with confidence intervals, the general-benchmark
regression, known failure modes, and the exact command that reproduces the run.
</Card>

A model card that reports only the wins is marketing. The one that gets you hired reports the MMLU
drop and the failure modes, because that is what a colleague needs in order to use your adapter
safely. Include the CPU/Colab fallback path in the README — the `standard` track must be completable
for $0.

## Rubric

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Correctness** | tests fail | `basic` passes | `standard` passes, &gt;99% schema-valid on held-out, merged and served | `hard` passes: rank sweep, Pareto plot, cost model, and a CI-backed win over the constrained baseline |
| **Code quality** | works | readable | config-driven, pinned versions, pre-flight checks, no magic constants | a reviewer would approve unchanged; assertions carry diagnostics; the retry and timeout policy is explicit |
| **Performance** | untested | trained once | VRAM, throughput and latency measured against a stated SLO | capacity plan derived from the KV formula and validated against a real run, with the prediction error reported |
| **Explanation** | none | a README | decisions documented, baseline and delta reported | every number has a CI; the model card names the regressions; the API-versus-self-host conclusion is defended with arithmetic |

**What a 3/3 on Explanation looks like:** you state which differences your eval cannot resolve. A
report that says "we cannot distinguish `q5_K_M` from `q4_K_M` at n=100; here is the sample size we
would need" demonstrates more statistical maturity than any point estimate, and it is the single
rarest thing in published quantization comparisons.

## If you get stuck

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Training runs but the model barely changes">
    In order: learning rate copied from full fine-tuning (needs 1e-4 to 3e-4, not 2e-5); training
    format not matching the chat template (print the tokenized string and diff it); base not
    actually frozen so you are silently doing something else; `max_seq_length` truncating most of
    your examples.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Schema-valid rate is high but F1 is bad">
    That is the expected result of constrained decoding alone, and it is the module's thesis rather
    than a bug. Your remaining errors are semantic. Check field order (does anything have to be
    emitted before its inputs?), add a `reasoning` field first, and look at whether the wrong values
    are fabrications for absent fields — measure groundedness.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="OOM during training on a 24 GB card">
    Check `print_trainable_parameters()` first — if it is not under 1%, the base is not frozen and
    that is the whole problem. Then: `gradient_checkpointing=True`, `per_device_train_batch_size`
    down with `gradient_accumulation_steps` up to compensate, `optim="paged_adamw_8bit"`, and
    `max_seq_length` set from your measured p99 rather than a round number.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="vLLM serves but the adapter output is wrong">
    Base revision drift is the usual cause — pin it and assert it. Then check you are requesting the
    adapter name in `model=`, not the base name; requesting the base silently serves the base with
    no error and no warning.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The cost model says self-hosting is worse">
    Then that is your finding, and it is a good one. Check your utilisation assumption is realistic
    (the war story in Module 5 is exactly this), report the accuracy gap that the cost saving trades
    against, and make the recommendation. Reaching an uncomfortable conclusion with sound arithmetic
    is the point of the `hard` track.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
