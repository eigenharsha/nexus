## Outcome

<Note>By Sunday you will be able to prove a model works — and, more valuably, to detect when a good-looking number is a lie. This is the rigor week. Everything in Weeks 17 and 18 produced numbers; this week decides which of them you are allowed to believe.</Note>

## The through-line

**M1** derives every classification metric from one four-cell table and shows that the metric you pick is a statement about which error costs more. Its central measurement: across a 289-fold change in prevalence, ROC-AUC never moves off 0.96 while precision at 90% recall falls **76-fold**, from 0.8418 to 0.0110. **M2** does the same for regression, ranking and probabilistic outputs — one outlier row multiplies MSE by **69** and the median absolute error by **1.00**; the MAPE-minimising forecast sits at **38.7% of the mean**; and two rankings that Recall@10 scores identically differ 0.9469 to 0.7328 on NDCG. It teaches Recall@k, MRR and NDCG properly because they are the metric set you will use to evaluate retrieval in Weeks 27 and 28.

**M3** turns to the *estimate* rather than the metric: a single hold-out has 3.6x the noise of a 5-fold mean, and choosing a split that does not mirror deployment inflates the number by **4.0 accuracy points** on grouped data and **15.3 AUC points** on a time series.

**M4** is the module that decides whether your career-defining model works. Nine distinct leaks, each measured: from a scaler fitted on all data at **+0.00** points, through target encoding on the full dataset at **+13.32**, up to feature selection using every row's label at **+33.50 points on data containing no signal at all.**

**M5** turns all of it into a document somebody can act on: baselines, slices, intervals, and a ship/no-ship recommendation with the assumption that would flip it named.

The habit this week installs is a specific one. **Before you believe a number, ask what it is an average over and how wide its interval is.** Every module is an instance of that question.

**The mnemonic to keep:** *recall reads a row, precision reads a column.* M1 draws it that way, and

denominator goes where.

## The two habits

**Write down the achievable range before you build.** "Published models on this task reach 0.72–0.78; anything above 0.85 is a bug until proven otherwise." One line in the project doc, zero cost, and it is the defence the £2M war story in M4 was missing.

**Never report a number without its interval and its slices.** A headline is an average over a population you probably do not care about, computed on one draw of a test set you could have drawn differently.
