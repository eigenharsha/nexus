## Outcome

<Note>By Sunday you will be able to turn raw data into features that move the metric, and to handle the 1000:1 class-ratio problems that dominate real business ML — including knowing when the standard advice for them is wrong.</Note>

## The through-line

**M1** encodes what you have. The encoder is a function of the model family — one-hot versus ordinal is worth **9.77 AUC points** to a logistic regression and **0.23** to a boosted tree — and target encoding has three implementations of which one inflates your score by **11.5 AUC points**, one makes the model *worse than omitting the feature*, and only the third is honest.

**M2** builds features from time and text. Its rule is one line long — every aggregate's window ends at the row's prediction timestamp — and breaking it is measured here costing **3.5 AUC points of promise and 2.3 points of real deliverable performance**.

**M3** throws most of them away. Cutting 200 features to 50 cost **0.01 AUC points**; the surprise is that model inference did not get faster, and the win was 175 upstream dependencies removed.

**M4** is the week's contrarian module. Four standard imbalance treatments, run head to head with bootstrap intervals: **every one significantly reduced average precision**, SMOTE by 11.5 points, and every one degraded calibration by up to 18.9x. The thing that worked was moving the threshold, driven by an explicit cost matrix.

**M5** ships it. One fitted object, and a demonstration of what a version bump does to it — one estimator crashed, one was fine, and one **loaded, ran, and returned probabilities differing by 0.152**.

The habit this week installs: **measure the standard advice before following it.** Three of the five modules contradict something widely believed, and each contradiction is a table you can reproduce.

## The three things this week contradicts

1. **"Use SMOTE for imbalanced data."** Measured: −11.5 average-precision points, interval excluding zero, and 9.2x worse calibration. Move the threshold instead.
2. **"Fewer features means faster serving."** Measured: 200 features to 25 changed inference from 1.47 to 1.83 µs/row. The win is upstream dependencies.
3. **"Smoothing fixes target encoding."** Measured: the leak persisted from `m=0` to `m=200`. Smoothing controls variance; out-of-fold controls leakage.

Each of those is a table you can regenerate in under ten minutes, which is the point — you are not being asked to take the contradiction on faith.
