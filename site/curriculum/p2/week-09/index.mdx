## Outcome

<Note>
By Sunday you will have built a 16-bit ALU and a register file out of nothing but NAND gates, and
you will be able to explain — with gate counts and nanoseconds, not adjectives — how `a + b` becomes
electricity.
</Note>

This is the week the floor of the abstraction stack becomes visible. Everything you have written so
far in this course sat on top of a machine you took on faith. After this week there is no faith left
in the stack below your code: you will have built it.

## What makes this week different

Weeks 1–8 taught you to build things. This week you take one apart, all the way down, and then
build it back up from a single part. There is one primitive — the NAND gate — and everything else in
the machine is a composition of copies of it. That includes arithmetic, memory, and the notion of
"next instruction".

Two threads run through all five modules, and both come back later:

1. **Depth, not gate count, sets the clock.** You will measure a 16-bit ripple-carry adder at 240
   NAND gates and 67 levels of depth, and a parallel-prefix adder at 503 gates and 20 levels. The
   second one is 2.1× the silicon and 3.35× faster, and that trade is the reason single-thread
   performance stopped doubling.
2. **The memory hierarchy is the performance model for the rest of your life.** L1 at ~1 ns, DRAM at
   ~90 ns, NVMe at ~100 µs. Module 4 makes you measure a **66.7× slowdown** on identical arithmetic
   caused by nothing but loop order, and Phase 4's FlashAttention is the same lesson at a different
   scale.

## Prerequisites

- Binary numbers and bitwise operators (Week 1). You should be comfortable with `0b1011`, `&`, `|`,
  `^`, `~` and shifts.
- A C toolchain (`clang` or `gcc`) for the Module 4 and 5 benchmarks. On macOS, `xcode-select
  --install`; on Linux, `build-essential`.
- Python 3.12 or 3.13 for the gate simulator. No third-party packages needed.
- Optional but recommended: the free Nand2Tetris hardware simulator from nand2tetris.org, for the
  official test scripts.

If you can produce that table from memory on Sunday, the week worked.

## What this unlocks

- **Week 10** — you now know what is at the bottom of a socket: a memory-mapped device and an
  interrupt.
- **Week 13** — you will stop believing that instruction count predicts runtime.
- **Phase 3** — SIMD widens to a GPU; the tensor core is this week's "one control signal, many copies
  of the arithmetic" argument, taken to 16,896 lanes.
- **Week 26** — "a 7B model in 4-bit weights needs about 3.5 GB" is arithmetic you can now do from
  first principles, because you know what a bit costs in silicon and what it costs to move.
