# You will not read about AI. You will build it.

Somewhere on your phone right now there is an AI that writes poems, answers questions, and
fixes code. Most people use it every day and have no idea how it works. A few people know how
it works *all the way down* — and those people are the ones companies fight to hire.

This course turns you into one of them. No previous knowledge needed. Every idea starts as a
small story, every hard idea gets a picture or an animation, and every word is explained
before it is used. You never sit there staring at a term you have never heard.

## The four phases

<Steps>
  <Step title="Phase 1 · Weeks 1–8 — Foundations" icon="code">
    How a computer really works: memory, the terminal, Python, how the web moves data, and
    databases that never lose it.
    **You finish with:** your own web service, tested and live on the internet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 2 · Weeks 9–16 — Systems & Data" icon="cpu">
    Build a tiny processor out of logic gates. Learn the small set of maths that all of AI
    stands on — taught gently, with pictures. Learn how data moves through real companies.
    **You finish with:** a working processor, a network server, your own maths library, and an
    automated data pipeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 3 · Weeks 17–24 — Machine Learning" icon="brain">
    Teach a machine to learn from examples — first by hand, so you truly understand it, then
    with the tools professionals use every day.
    **You finish with:** your own trained model, running as a real service, defended in a
    mid-course review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 4 · Weeks 25–32 — Generative AI" icon="sparkles">
    The AI everyone talks about, from the inside: how chat models read and write, how to give
    them your documents and your tools, how to test them, and how to run them for real users.
    **You finish with:** a complete AI system of your own — and you can explain every part of it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The honest requirements

You need a computer, roughly 12–15 hours a week if you are a full-time student, or 5–7 hours a
week if you have a job, and the willingness to be bad at something for a while. You do not
need any programming experience. You do not need a maths degree. You do not need an expensive
graphics card — every lesson has a free path, and the whole 34 weeks costs under $50 in cloud
spend.
