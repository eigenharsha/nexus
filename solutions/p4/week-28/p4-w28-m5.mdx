## Reference solution — `P4-W28-M5`

### The task

Measure hit rate, latency saving, dollar saving and false-hit rate across five thresholds; recommend
one and state the risk.

### The solution that works

The code is Module 5 Layer 2's `SemanticCache`. The part that cannot be automated is the labelling,
and the sampling design decides whether the number means anything:

```python
# stratify by similarity band — nearly all the risk is near the threshold
bands = {"0.97-0.98": [], "0.98-0.99": [], "0.99-1.00": []}
# ... sample evenly from each, then a human answers ONE question per hit:
#     "would this cached answer be correct for THIS query?"
```

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious recommendation maximises hit rate subject to "the answers look fine".** They do look
fine — that is the danger. In the war story, 9.5% of sampled semantic hits were wrong and **one user
in nineteen reported it**. False hits are silent, fluent and carry citations. The only way to know
the rate is to label a sample by hand, and the only way to make the number trustworthy is to
stratify the sample toward the threshold where the risk concentrates.

**The obvious recommendation also omits the risk statement.** "We use a 0.97 threshold" is a
configuration note. "Approximately 0.16% of all answers will be wrong for the question asked; we
judge this acceptable for support search with visible citations and disable semantic caching for any
query mentioning an account, a price or an identifier" is a decision a product owner can accept or
reject.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: TTL as invalidation.** A 24-hour TTL means serving stale answers for up to 24 hours
after a document changes, and it cannot evict precisely. The reverse index from chunk ID to cache
keys — populated from the answer's own citation set — is about fifteen lines and it makes
invalidation exact. The test that catches the difference is "answers citing *other* documents must
NOT be evicted", which a TTL-based flush fails.

**Wrong turn 2: the RediSearch distance/similarity inversion.** `FT.SEARCH` with a KNN clause returns
*distance*, not similarity. Treating it as similarity inverts your threshold, so a 0.97 threshold
becomes "serve anything with cosine below 0.03" — which caches essentially nothing and looks like the
cache is simply not working. Assert that an identical query returns similarity 1.0.

### Grading notes

**3/3** requires hand-labelled false-hit rates at four or more thresholds with stratified sampling,
the entity gate measured separately, citation-based invalidation proven by test, the cost waterfall,
and a risk statement in product terms. **The three most common point losses:** a false-hit rate
estimated rather than labelled — usually reported as 0%, which is the tell; TTL presented as
invalidation; and a recommendation with no risk statement, which leaves the product decision
implicitly made by the engineer.
