The two files reviewers open first are `DESIGN.md` and `chaos/report.md`. Make them the best two
files in the repository.

### The five things that separate a 3/3 from a 2/3

1. **`chaos/report.md` shows 20 kills, `duplicate_effects: 0`, and the replay cost overhead as a number.** Not "it survived" — the table.
2. **The evidence reducer de-duplicates.** A plain append passes every happy-path test and produces duplicated context the first time a node replays. This is the single most common defect in submissions.
3. **Read and write MCP servers are separate processes with separate credentials.** Show the two role definitions. A reviewer can check this in ten seconds and it tells them whether you understood Week 29 M4.
4. **The blast-radius table's "what stops it" column names code.** Grep your own table for the word "prompt". Every occurrence is a row to fix.
5. **A conclusion about the critic, either way.** "We added a critic and it improved quality by 0.31 points at 1.8× cost, so we enabled it only for P1 incidents" and "we added a critic, it changed quality by 0.04 points, we removed it" are both 3/3. "We added a critic" is 1.

### The MTTR table that belongs in your README

| | Median TTR | Context-gathering share | Model spend / incident |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human only (baseline) | 41 min | 19 min | — |
| Agent at suggest level | 33 min | 4 min | $0.21 |
| Agent at approve level | 28 min | 4 min | $0.24 |
| Agent auto for 3 of 11 action kinds | 26 min | 4 min | $0.24 |

Reading this table is the point of the lab: **most of the win arrives before any autonomy over
actions**, and the last two rows carry nearly all of the risk for 2 of the 15 minutes saved.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**Obvious:** start at approve level because suggest "does not do anything". Suggest is where 8 of
the 15 minutes come from, it is unarguable in a design review, and it generates the agreement data
that every later autonomy conversation needs.

**Obvious:** one graph node per agent role. Roles and nodes are different axes. `gather` is one node
that runs an analyst agent over several tools; `propose` is one node that runs a planner agent. Six
to ten nodes, three roles.

**Obvious:** treat the runbook corpus as static input. It is the highest-leverage thing in the
system and it should be owned, versioned and improved by the on-call rota — which is also the fix
for the "nobody owns the prompts" failure that kills these projects.

## Grading notes

**Automatic caps.** No `chaos/report.md` → maximum 60. No blast-radius table → maximum 70. Any
unmeasured claim in the README → maximum 75. A duplicate side effect under chaos → the Durability
row is 0 and the lab does not pass.

**A 3/3 submission** reads as an engineering report with a recorded demo attached: the ticket, the
design and its blast radius, the measurements, the chaos evidence, and an honest section on what you
would fix next. The recorded 90-second kill-and-resume demo is what people actually watch, and it
takes ten minutes to make.

**The three most common losses:** a plain-append evidence reducer; a per-process idempotency key that
looks correct and produces duplicates under chaos; and a design document with an architecture
section and no operations section.
