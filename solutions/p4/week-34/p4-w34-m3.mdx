## Reference solution — `P4-W34-M3`

### Two rows make the submission

Every cost report has a descending waterfall. A 3/3 report has two additional rows that most do not.

**The row that costs more per run.** Verify-and-retry: +$0.008 per run, success 75.8% → 86.1%, cost
per successful task $0.100 → $0.098. Anyone optimizing cost per call deletes this change. It is the
best change in the table.

**The counter-example.** Route everything to the cheap model: $0.058 per run (a 69% saving on the
invoice) and 54.0% success, giving **$0.107 per successful task** — worse than the $0.098 we shipped,
and far worse in product terms. Put this row in front of anyone proposing a blanket downgrade.

### The full waterfall

| Step | Cost/run | Success | Cost per successful task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | $0.190 | 75.0% | $0.253 |
| Prompt caching | $0.143 | 75.0% | $0.191 |
| Route triage + verify to haiku | $0.128 | 74.2% | $0.173 |
| Evidence digests, not full text | $0.104 | 75.0% | $0.139 |
| Semantic cache (31% hit, 1.2% false hits) | $0.076 | 75.8% | $0.100 |
| **Verify-and-retry** | $0.084 | 86.1% | **$0.098** |

−61%, and every row was checked against the 120-case suite. Six of eleven configurations we measured
turned out to be **dominated** — something else was both cheaper and better — which is the normal
state of an unexamined system.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**Obvious:** switch models first, because it is the biggest single number. It is the most visible
lever and the one most likely to raise cost per successful task. Free wins first: caching (25% for
five lines, zero quality risk), trimming (13%, and it slightly *improves* quality by removing
distractors), then per-node routing measured against the suite.

**Obvious:** route by model tier globally. Route per node. Haiku matched Opus on 118 of 120 triage
cases and 71 of 120 diagnosis cases — the rule that emerged is *constrained-choice steps move,
free-form judgement steps do not.*

**Obvious:** treat a semantic cache as free money. It is a classifier with a false-hit rate — ours
1.2% at a 0.93 threshold — and it needs a sweep, a stated budget and a TTL matched to how fast your
domain goes stale. Incidents go stale in thirty minutes.

## The two wrong turns worth knowing about

1. **A cost function reading only `input_tokens`.** Cache creation and cache read are separate usage fields at 1.25× and 0.1×. Our dashboard was 40% low for two weeks and, of course, in the flattering direction.
2. **Optimizing the node we found annoying rather than the node with the money.** `diagnose` was 34% of spend and `triage` was 8%; the team's instinct was to fix triage because its prompt was ugly. Attribute first, with a p95 column — ours showed `diagnose` p95 at 3.3× its mean, which turned out to be a retry loop, i.e. a correctness bug wearing a cost costume.

## Grading notes

**3/3 on Cost:** per-step attribution with p95, at least 50% reduction in cost per successful task,
an eval check on every row, the row that costs more per run, and the measured counter-example.

**Automatic cap:** a report whose headline is cost per call caps at 75, whatever else it contains.

**Common losses:** cache tokens unpriced; a blanket model downgrade with no per-node measurement; a
semantic cache with no false-hit rate; and — most common — a waterfall with no eval check on the
intermediate rows, so "no quality regression" is asserted rather than measured.
