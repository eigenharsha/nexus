## Reference solution — `P4-W34-M5`

### Version the quadruple, or "what changed" is unanswerable

Code sha, prompt version, pinned model version, retrieval config, eval suite version. One ConfigMap,
one review, and all of them in every API response and on every trace. If a submission has four of
the five, the missing one is almost always the model version — pinned as an alias, which is an
undeclared deploy run by someone else.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**Obvious:** use the model alias so you always get the latest. That is an undeclared deploy on
someone else's schedule, and it caused Module 4's four-day incident. Pin, and upgrade deliberately
with the suite in front of you.

**Obvious:** gate the canary on latency and error rate, like a normal service. Neither moves during
the failure that matters. Three gates: the offline suite, an online quality metric, and cost per run
— and each of ours stopped exactly one rollout in fourteen.

**Obvious:** the eval suite makes model migration free. It made ours six days instead of a quarter of
anxiety. Budget the week; remember your judge is a model too and needs re-validating; and re-check
every number derived from token counts.

## The two wrong turns worth knowing about

1. **Default `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` of 30 s against a 180 s maximum run.** Every deploy killed work in flight. Checkpointing rescued it, which is why nobody noticed for weeks — and why "it seems fine" is not evidence.
2. **A prompt that grew for eight months and was never rewritten.** 640 to 2,180 tokens, most of it patches for failure modes three model versions old. Rewriting from scratch against the suite took it to 810 tokens and *improved* the score by 2 points. Budget a rewrite every six months; the suite is the specification it must satisfy.

## Grading notes

**3/3 on Maintainability:** a TCO including engineer-days, a deprecation plan with a per-migration
estimate, the three kinds of drift with their detection signals, and named answers to the four
organizational questions — plus the fifth: who decides to turn it off, and on what condition.

**3/3 on Communication:** the poster with your own measured numbers written on the components, and a
five-minute presentation that covers what problem, what it decides autonomously, one number proving
it works, one number proving you can afford it, and what breaks first.

**Common losses:** an alias instead of a pin; a TCO with no people line; a poster with generic boxes
and no numbers; and no answer to "who owns this in eight months", which is the question that decides
whether any of the rest mattered.
