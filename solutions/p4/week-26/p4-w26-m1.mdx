## Reference solution — `P4-W26-M1`

### The task

Benchmark five quantization levels on VRAM/size, tokens/sec and a 50-question eval; plot the Pareto
frontier with confidence intervals and choose a default.

### The solution that works

The harness is Module 1 Layer 2's `bench_quant.py`. The part that separates a 3/3 from a 1/3 is
this addition:

```python
from scipy.stats import binomtest

def score_with_ci(correct: int, n: int):
    ci = binomtest(correct, n).proportion_ci(confidence_level=0.95)
    return correct / n, ci.low, ci.high

for q, correct in results.items():
    p, lo, hi = score_with_ci(correct, 50)
    print(f"{q:8} {p:.2f}  95% CI [{lo:.2f}, {hi:.2f}]  width {hi-lo:.2f}")
```

And the sample-size calculation that tells you what your eval can actually resolve:

```python
# to resolve a difference of `delta` at p~0.82 with 80% power, two-proportion z-test
from math import ceil
p, delta, z_a, z_b = 0.82, 0.04, 1.96, 0.84
n = ceil(2 * p * (1 - p) * (z_a + z_b) ** 2 / delta ** 2)
print(n)     # 725 per arm
```

### Measured numbers this produces

M2 Pro (200 GB/s), Ollama 0.3.11, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct:

| Quant | Size | tok/s | roofline | achieved | Score /50 | 95% CI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `q8_0` | 8.54 GB | 20.1 | 23.4 | 86% | 43 | [0.72, 0.94] |
| `q5_K_M` | 5.73 GB | 30.1 | 34.9 | 86% | 42 | [0.70, 0.93] |
| `q4_K_M` | 4.92 GB | 35.2 | 40.6 | 87% | 41 | [0.68, 0.92] |
| `q3_K_M` | 4.02 GB | 40.8 | 49.8 | 82% | 36 | [0.58, 0.85] |
| `q2_K` | 3.18 GB | 48.5 | 62.9 | 77% | 27 | [0.40, 0.69] |

**Default: `q4_K_M`.** It is 42% smaller and 75% faster than `q8_0`, and the eval cannot
distinguish them — you would need about 725 items per arm to resolve a 4-point difference at 80%
power. The only differences this eval *can* resolve are `q3_K_M` and `q2_K` being worse.

### Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious solution reports point estimates and ranks them.** With 50 items, the CI width is
about 0.22 — wider than every difference in the top four rows. Ranking on point estimates is
ranking on noise, and the fact that this is the standard format for online quantization comparisons
does not make it valid.

**The second obvious mistake is not computing the roofline column.** Without it you cannot see the
genuinely interesting finding: achieved-versus-predicted throughput is flat at 86% down to 4 bits
and then *falls* to 77% at `q2_K`. Below 4 bits you are spending compute on dequantization to save
bandwidth, which is the wrong direction on this hardware. That is a real result and it is invisible
in a plain tokens/sec column.

### The two wrong turns worth knowing about

**Wrong turn 1: benchmarking with the model cold.** The first request after `ollama pull` includes
loading 4.9 GB from disk. Measured: first request 3.1 s TTFT, subsequent 0.09 s. Discard the first
three requests of every configuration, and say so in your write-up.

**Wrong turn 2: an eval with no capability stratification.** The war story in Layer 2 is that
aggregate fluency stayed flat while numeric fidelity fell from 96.7% to 83.3%. Split your 50
questions into at least two capability buckets before you conclude anything — and if a bucket has
12 items, its CI is ±0.28 and you should say so rather than quietly reporting the point estimate.

### Grading notes

**3/3** requires CIs on every score, the roofline column, capability stratification, and an explicit
sentence naming which differences the eval cannot resolve. **The three most common point losses:**
ranking quantization levels on a 50-item point estimate; reporting tokens/sec without the roofline
comparison, which hides the sub-4-bit inflection; and benchmarking cold, which inflates the
first-measured configuration by up to 3 seconds of TTFT.
