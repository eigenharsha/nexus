### The cost waterfall that belongs in your README

| Step | Cost/run | Success | Cost per successful task | Eval delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | $0.190 | 75.0% | $0.253 | — |
| Prompt caching | $0.143 | 75.0% | $0.191 | 0 |
| Route triage + verify to a cheap model | $0.128 | 74.2% | $0.173 | −0.8 (within noise) |
| Evidence digests | $0.104 | 75.0% | $0.139 | +0.4 |
| Semantic cache | $0.076 | 75.8% | $0.100 | +0.8 |
| **Verify-and-retry** | **$0.084** | **86.1%** | **$0.098** | **+10.3** |
| *(counter-example)* all-cheap | $0.058 | 54.0% | $0.107 | −21.0 |

**−61%**, with the eval delta on every row. The last two rows are what make it a 3/3 rather than a
2/3: one lever that costs more per run and less per task, and one measured demonstration that the
cheapest per call is the most expensive per outcome.

### The five things that separate a 3/3 from a 2/3

1. **A test proving the traces are trees.** One trace id, one root, at least eight spans. Nine lines, and it catches the bug that wastes weeks.
2. **`cache_read_input_tokens` asserted non-zero** in a test, and cache tokens priced at 1.25× and 0.1×. A cost report that misses these is 40% low.
3. **The game day run blind.** A colleague picks the condition and the time. Ours went from 19 minutes to three hours, and that number is more informative than the first one.
4. **A TCO with engineer-days in it.** 21% of ours. It is the line that decides whether the system exists in a year.
5. **The poster carries your own numbers.** Recall on the retrieval box, kappa on the judge, cost per successful task on the model box, TTFT on the response arrow. A component with no number is one nobody measured.

### Six dashboard tiles, and no more

Success rate · TTFT p50/p95 · cost per successful task · groundedness p10 · offline/online gap ·
unreviewed eval candidates.

Resist adding more. A dashboard nobody reads is worse than no dashboard, because it creates the
belief that someone is watching. Every tile should have a person who would notice it moving.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**Obvious:** measure cost per call, because that is what the invoice divides into. It is the number
that let a team in Module 3's war story cut spend 47% and raise cost per resolved ticket by 19%.

**Obvious:** alert on everything important. Route by *urgency*, not importance. An alert that pages
for a slow-moving quality trend gets muted within a month and takes the fast-moving ones with it.
Half of ours are tickets.

**Obvious:** the poster is a documentation task at the end. It is a diagnostic. Drawing it is how you
discover which components you never measured, and it takes an hour, and it should be drawn before
the final week rather than after.

## Grading notes

**Automatic caps.** No `OPERATIONS.md` → maximum 60. No TCO → maximum 70. Cost per call as the
headline anywhere → maximum 75. A cost reduction with no eval check on the intermediate rows →
maximum 75.

**A 3/3 submission** is an operations report: what the system does, what it costs per successful
task with the waterfall, how you know it works, what happened during the game day including the
condition you missed, what it costs to own including people, and one page that shows all of it with
real numbers on it. It takes longer to write than the code and it is the part that gets read.

**The three most common losses:** a forest of single-span traces; a waterfall whose rows have no
eval check; and a TCO consisting only of the API line, which is the 71% everyone already knew about.
