## Reference solution — `P3-W19-M2`

The task: build a reliability diagram for an XGBoost model, calibrate it, show the improvement — and then show that fixing the model beats calibrating it.

## The three-way split, and why it is not optional

```
train        -> fit the model
calibration  -> fit the calibrator          <- the model has NEVER seen these rows
test         -> report everything
```

Fitting the calibrator on training residuals teaches it how the model behaves on data it has memorised, which is not how it behaves on new data. In scikit-learn 1.9 the mechanism is:

```python
from sklearn.calibration import CalibratedClassifierCV
from sklearn.frozen import FrozenEstimator          # cv="prefit" was removed in 1.6

cal = CalibratedClassifierCV(FrozenEstimator(model), method="sigmoid").fit(X_cal, y_cal)
```

A submission still passing `cv="prefit"` will fail on 1.9 with `InvalidParameterError`, and that is a useful thing to have hit once.

## The reliability diagram

Use **quantile** bins, not equal-width ones:

```python
frac, mean = calibration_curve(y_test, p, n_bins=10, strategy="quantile")
```

Equal-width bins on a confident classifier put nearly every row in the first and last bin and leave the middle eight nearly empty, so the plot shows two points and eight artifacts. Equal-count bins put the same number of rows in each and the diagram becomes readable. This single argument is the difference between a diagnostic and a decoration.

Summarise it with an ECE-style number — the mean absolute gap between predicted and observed across bins — so the improvement is a scalar you can put in a table.

## The measured result

| | Brier | Log-loss | ROC-AUC | ECE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| raw booster | 0.17520 | 0.66588 | 0.8061 | **0.1366** |
| + Platt | 0.1554 | — | 0.8061 | **0.0188** |
| + isotonic | 0.1342 | — | — | — |

**ECE fell by 86%. ROC-AUC did not move at all.** Both are the expected result and both belong in the write-up: calibration is a monotone rescaling of the score, so it cannot change the ranking, and any submission reporting an AUC change of more than about 0.001 under Platt has a bug.

## The part most submissions miss

The task says: *then show that fixing the model beats calibrating it.*

Calibration is a patch applied after the fact. It corrects the mapping from score to probability; it cannot add information. So the honest comparison has three arms, not two:

1. **Raw model.**
2. **Raw model + calibrator.**
3. **A model trained not to need one** — more trees at a lower learning rate, `min_child_weight` raised, or simply early stopping on log-loss rather than on AUC.

Arm 3 usually wins on log-loss *and* on discrimination, because the same over-confidence that wrecks calibration is a symptom of over-fitting the training data. Calibration hides the symptom; regularisation removes the cause.

**The recommendation that follows:** calibrate when you cannot retrain (a vendor model, a frozen artifact, a fixed compute budget), and retrain when you can. Reaching for `CalibratedClassifierCV` first is treating a diagnostic as a fix.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious solution calibrates, reports that Brier improved, and stops.** Two problems. Brier is frequently the metric that moves *least* — it decomposes as reliability minus resolution plus uncertainty (Murphy, 1973), calibration only touches the reliability term, and on a well-discriminating model that term is small. A submission that reports an unchanged Brier as a failure has not understood its own metric. Report **log-loss and ECE** as the calibration-sensitive numbers and Brier as the combined one.

Two more wrong turns:

1. **Equal-width bins.** Produces a diagram that looks broken and an ECE dominated by two bins holding 90% of the rows.
2. **Isotonic on a small calibration set.** Isotonic regression fits a step function with as many degrees of freedom as it needs; below roughly 1,000 calibration points it over-fits and does worse than Platt's two parameters. Report both and pick on the calibration-set size, saying which and why.

## Grading notes

**3/3 looks like:** a genuine three-way split with `FrozenEstimator`; quantile-binned reliability curves before and after, on the same axes; a table with Brier, log-loss, AUC and ECE for raw, Platt and isotonic; an explicit statement that AUC is unchanged and why; and the third arm — a retrained model — showing that fixing the cause beats patching the symptom.

**The three most common ways submissions lose points:**

1. **Calibrating on training or test data.** On training it under-corrects; on test the reported improvement is circular.
2. **Reporting only Brier.** It is the metric least likely to show what you did.
3. **Stopping at two arms.** The task asks for three, and the third is the one with the practical recommendation in it.
