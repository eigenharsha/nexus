## Reference solution — `P3-W18-M3`

The task: run 30+ configurations, report the sensitivity ranking, and defend a default recipe.

## The protocol

Fixed before any fit, and this ordering is most of the marks:

- Three-way split — 36,000 train / 12,000 validation / 12,000 test, stratified, `random_state=0`
- `eval_metric="logloss"`, `early_stopping_rounds=50`, `n_estimators=3000` as a ceiling
- Baseline: `learning_rate=0.05`, `max_depth=6`, `subsample=0.8`, `colsample_bytree=0.8`, `min_child_weight=1`, `reg_lambda=1.0`, `gamma=0.0`, `tree_method="hist"`
- One parameter varied at a time, everything else at baseline
- Report test AUC, `best_iteration` and wall clock for every run

Baseline: **test AUC 0.98249**, 1,150 trees, 4.1 s.

`max_depth` moves the metric **17x more than `learning_rate`**. Ranks 3 through 7 span a total of 0.00089 AUC between them, which on 12,000 test rows is at or below the seed-to-seed noise — running the baseline at five seeds is what tells you that, and a submission that skips it cannot honestly call anything below rank 2 a result.

Two of the ranges are also *asymmetric* in an important way. `max_depth`'s range is driven almost entirely by one bad value (2, at 0.97306); depths 4 through 12 span only 0.0008. `gamma`'s is driven entirely by `gamma=10` (0.97764); 0 to 2 spans 0.0004. **Both of the "sensitive" parameters are sensitive only to being set badly.** That is a different claim from "these parameters are worth tuning", and stating the difference is the highest-value sentence in the write-up.

## The recipe, and its defence

```python
xgb.XGBClassifier(
    n_estimators=5000,        # a ceiling. early stopping picks the real number
    learning_rate=0.05,       # a TIME knob: 0.3 while iterating, 0.02 for the final fit
    max_depth=6,              # tune this first, over {4, 6, 8, 10}. it is the only one that matters
    min_child_weight=5,       # measured best of {1,5,20,100}; raise on noisy data
    subsample=0.8,            # below the noise floor here; kept for variance on other data
    colsample_bytree=0.8,     # same
    reg_lambda=1.0,           # same. the default is fine
    gamma=0.0,                # do not raise it without measuring; 10 cost 0.005 AUC
    tree_method="hist",       # 7.3x faster than exact and marginally more accurate
    eval_metric="aucpr",      # match the business metric, not the library default
    early_stopping_rounds=50,
    random_state=0, n_jobs=-1)
```

Distance from the best configuration anywhere in the 31 runs (`max_depth=8`, 0.98313): **0.00064 AUC**, at 4.1 s against 3.5 s. Adding a four-value `max_depth` sweep on top closes even that, for a total of five fits.

## Why this solution and not the obvious one

**The obvious solution runs a `GridSearchCV` over a 5-parameter grid and reports the best configuration.** Three things go wrong. A 5-parameter grid at 4 values each is 1,024 fits, which at 4 s each is over an hour for information that five one-at-a-time sweeps give you in three minutes. It gives you the argmax but not the *sensitivity*, which is the thing that changes your behaviour next time. And with early stopping the fits have wildly different costs, so a grid spends most of its time in the slow corner of the space for no reason.

Two more wrong turns worth knowing about:

1. **Early stopping on the test set.** The single most common error in a boosting study. It makes every reported number optimistic by an amount that grows with how much you tuned, and it is invisible because the model still looks fine. Three-way split, and assert in code that the `eval_set` array is not the test array.
2. **Not measuring the noise floor.** Without five baseline seeds, a difference of 0.0004 looks like a finding. With them it is obviously not. Every parameter below rank 2 in this study is inside the noise, and reporting them as a ranking without that caveat is the difference between a study and a table.

## Grading notes

**3/3 looks like:** the protocol is written down before the first fit; 30+ configurations with AUC, `best_iteration` and wall clock for each; the seed-noise floor measured and drawn on the sensitivity chart; the observation that `max_depth` and `gamma` are sensitive only at their bad extremes; and a recipe with a per-parameter justification and a measured distance from the best result.

**The three most common ways submissions lose points:**

1. **No noise floor.** Turns a study into a list of numbers with no way to tell signal from wobble.
2. **Early stopping on the reporting set.** Silently inflates everything; costs a correctness point regardless of how good the analysis is.
3. **Concluding "tune the learning rate".** The measurement says the opposite: 14x the fit time for 0.0006 AUC. Reporting the folklore when your own data contradicts it is the failure this module is designed to catch.
